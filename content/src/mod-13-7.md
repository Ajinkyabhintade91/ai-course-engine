# AI Project Portfolio Checklist

Module 11.5 explained why a portfolio wins clients and how to build the first pieces. This bonus module is the **checklist** you run over every piece before a prospect sees it: what each piece must contain, how to present the result and prove it, and how to keep the whole portfolio current. A portfolio is not a gallery of things you built; it is evidence that you solve problems like the prospect's. Every item on the checklist exists to make that evidence easier to believe.

Examples use Sam's three pieces: the Harper Joinery quote assistant, an invoice reminder workflow, and an FAQ chatbot. Thresholds such as 180 days are illustrative starting points.

## 1. First-Principles Mental Model & Intuition

### The joiner's showroom

A joiner's showroom does not show every cabinet ever made. It shows a few finished pieces, each with a card: what the customer wanted, what was built, a photo of it fitted, and a line from the customer. Damaged or dated samples are repaired or removed, because one shabby piece makes a visitor doubt the rest. A digital portfolio works the same way: a few complete, current pieces beat many half-finished ones.

```text
 EACH PIECE                       PRESENTATION AND PROOF               MAINTENANCE
  problem (their words)            headline: before -> after (%)        check every 90 days
  result (before and after)        proof score:                         fix broken demo links
  demo (working link)                metric 3, testimonial 2,           refresh pieces older
  client quote                       demo 2, code 1, screenshot 1        than 180 days
  two kinds of evidence            rank pieces: lead with strongest     turn gaps into actions
  updated in the last 180 days
```

### Why a checklist

When you look at your own work, you see what you remember about it, not what is on the page. A checklist forces you to see what a stranger sees: is the problem stated, is there a number, does the link work, does someone other than you vouch for it? Pilots and surgeons use checklists not because they lack skill but because skill does not stop people forgetting the obvious under time pressure, and portfolio pieces are usually written in a hurry at the end of a project.

## 2. Core Theory, Mathematical Foundations & Architecture Internals

### The six-item checklist for each piece

Every piece is checked against six items:

| Item | Passes when | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | stated, ideally in the client's own words | the prospect recognises their own pain |
| Result | a before and an after measurement exist | numbers are believable and comparable |
| Demo | a link exists and works | seeing it run beats any description |
| Quote | a client testimonial is present | someone other than you vouches for it |
| Evidence | at least two kinds of evidence | one kind can be dismissed; two corroborate |
| Updated | reviewed within the last 180 days | stale pieces suggest stale skills |

A piece's checklist score is the number of items passed, from 0 to 6. Sam's Harper Joinery piece passes all six. The invoice reminder workflow has a problem, a result, and two kinds of evidence but no demo, no quote, and was last updated 260 days ago, so it scores 3. The FAQ chatbot has only a recent update date, scoring 1.

### Presentation: the result headline

The first line a prospect reads should be the result, not the technology. A headline states the before, the after, and the improvement:

$$\text{improvement} = \operatorname{round}\!\left(100 \times \frac{\text{before} - \text{after}}{\text{before}}\right)\%$$

for measures where lower is better, such as minutes per quote or days to payment. Harper Joinery's quoting went from 180 to 20 minutes, an 89% improvement; the invoice workflow cut days to payment from 41 to 18, 56%. A piece without both measurements gets no headline, which is itself a signal to go back and measure. For measures where higher is better, such as quotes sent per week, swap the roles of before and after in the formula, or present the multiple, "three times as many quotes".

### Proof: scoring the evidence

Not all evidence is equally persuasive. A simple weighting:

| Evidence | Weight | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Metric | 3 | a measured before and after is hardest to argue with |
| Testimonial | 2 | an independent voice, especially with a name and business |
| Demo | 2 | the prospect can see it working |
| Code | 1 | convincing to technical buyers, opaque to most others |
| Screenshot | 1 | shows it exists, but is easy to stage |

The proof score is the sum of weights over the distinct kinds of evidence a piece has. Harper Joinery has a metric, a testimonial, and a screenshot: 6. The invoice workflow has a metric and a screenshot: 4. The FAQ chatbot has only a screenshot: 1. Counting distinct kinds stops ten screenshots outscoring one measured result.

Testimonials carry rules. In the UK, the Advertising Standards Authority expects testimonials to be genuine, attributable, and held on file; in the US, the Federal Trade Commission's endorsement guides, 16 CFR Part 255, require that endorsements reflect the endorser's honest experience and that material connections, such as a discount given in exchange, are disclosed. Ask clients in writing, keep their reply, and never edit a quote beyond trimming it.

### Ranking: which piece leads

A prospect gives a portfolio about a minute. Lead with the strongest piece, ideally one close to their industry. A simple combined score weights completeness and proof:

$$\text{rank score} = 2 \times \text{checklist score} + \text{proof score}$$

Harper Joinery: $2 \times 6 + 6 = 18$; invoice workflow: $2 \times 3 + 4 = 10$; FAQ chatbot: $2 \times 1 + 1 = 3$. Ties are broken alphabetically for a stable order. In practice Sam may also move a piece up when it matches the prospect's industry, Module 11.4, but the score sets the default.

### Maintenance: turning gaps into actions

Each missing checklist item maps to a concrete action: write the problem in the client's words, measure before and after, fix or add the demo link, ask the client for a quote, add a second kind of evidence, or review and update the piece. The portfolio is reviewed on a fixed cycle, every 90 days by default, and the next review date is set when the current one ends. Broken links are the commonest decay: hosted demos expire, free tiers are withdrawn, and pages move, a problem widely studied as **link rot**.

### Complexity

Checking a piece, writing its headline, and scoring its proof are constant-time for a fixed checklist; ranking $n$ pieces is $O(n \log n)$; the maintenance list is linear in the number of pieces.

## 3. Production-Grade Implementation & Annotated Walkthrough

First, the pieces and the checklist.

```python
from datetime import date, timedelta

PIECES = [
    {"name": "Harper Joinery quote assistant", "problem": "Quotes took three hours", "before": 180, "after": 20,
     "unit": "minutes per quote", "demo": "https://example.com/harper-demo", "link_ok": True,
     "quote": "We now quote the same day.", "evidence": ["metric", "testimonial", "screenshot"], "updated": "2026-11-10"},
    {"name": "Invoice reminder workflow", "problem": "Invoices were paid late", "before": 41, "after": 18,
     "unit": "days to payment", "demo": "", "link_ok": True,
     "quote": "", "evidence": ["metric", "screenshot"], "updated": "2026-03-02"},
    {"name": "FAQ chatbot", "problem": "", "before": None, "after": None,
     "unit": "", "demo": "https://example.com/faq", "link_ok": False,
     "quote": "", "evidence": ["screenshot"], "updated": "2026-06-20"},
]
CHECKLIST = ["problem", "result", "demo", "quote", "evidence", "updated"]

def check_piece(piece, today, max_age=180):
    ok = {
        "problem": bool(piece["problem"]),
        "result": piece["before"] is not None and piece["after"] is not None,
        "demo": bool(piece["demo"]) and piece["link_ok"],
        "quote": bool(piece["quote"]),
        "evidence": len(set(piece["evidence"])) >= 2,
        "updated": (date.fromisoformat(today) - date.fromisoformat(piece["updated"])).days <= max_age,
    }
    missing = [c for c in CHECKLIST if not ok[c]]
    return len(CHECKLIST) - len(missing), missing

for p in PIECES:
    print(p["name"], check_piece(p, "2026-11-17"))
```

- **Harper Joinery: 6 and nothing missing.**
- **Invoice workflow: 3, missing demo, quote, and updated;** 260 days have passed since 2 March.
- **FAQ chatbot: 1;** a demo link exists but is broken, so the demo item fails too.
- **The dictionary of checks** keeps each rule on one line, so adding a seventh item is a one-line change plus an entry in `CHECKLIST`.

Next, the headline.

```python
def headline(piece):
    if piece["before"] is None or piece["after"] is None:
        return None
    pct = round(100 * (piece["before"] - piece["after"]) / piece["before"])
    return f"{piece['name']}: {piece['before']} to {piece['after']} {piece['unit']} ({pct}% better)"

print(headline(PIECES[0]))
print(headline(PIECES[2]))
```

- **"Harper Joinery quote assistant: 180 to 20 minutes per quote (89% better)".**
- **No headline for the FAQ chatbot,** because nothing was measured.

Then, the proof score.

```python
WEIGHTS = {"metric": 3, "testimonial": 2, "demo": 2, "code": 1, "screenshot": 1}

def proof_score(piece):
    return sum(WEIGHTS.get(e, 0) for e in set(piece["evidence"]))

print([proof_score(p) for p in PIECES])
```

- **6, 4, and 1.**
- **`set` counts each kind once,** and unknown kinds score zero rather than raising an error.

Then, ranking the pieces.

```python
def rank(pieces, today):
    rows = [(p["name"], 2 * check_piece(p, today)[0] + proof_score(p)) for p in pieces]
    return sorted(rows, key=lambda r: (-r[1], r[0]))

print(rank(PIECES, "2026-11-17"))
```

- **Harper Joinery 18, invoice workflow 10, FAQ chatbot 3.** Sam leads with Harper Joinery and considers hiding the chatbot until it is fixed.

Finally, maintenance.

```python
ACTIONS = {"problem": "write the problem in the client's words", "result": "measure before and after",
           "demo": "fix or add the demo link", "quote": "ask the client for a quote",
           "evidence": "add a second kind of evidence", "updated": "review and update the piece"}

def maintenance(pieces, today, every=90):
    actions = [f"{p['name']}: {ACTIONS[m]}" for p in pieces for m in check_piece(p, today)[1]]
    nxt = (date.fromisoformat(today) + timedelta(days=every)).isoformat()
    return actions, nxt

actions, nxt = maintenance(PIECES, "2026-11-17")
print(len(actions), actions[0], nxt)
```

- **Eight actions,** three for the invoice workflow and five for the chatbot, starting with "Invoice reminder workflow: fix or add the demo link".
- **The next review is 15 February 2027,** 90 days on.

**Complexity.** Linear in the number of pieces apart from the ranking sort.

### Sam's portfolio routine

```text
AFTER EACH PROJECT  measure before and after; ask for a quote in writing; save screenshots; record a demo
BEFORE A PITCH      run check_piece on every piece; lead with the highest rank, adjusted for industry
EVERY 90 DAYS       run maintenance; fix links; refresh or retire pieces older than 180 days
```

## 4. Practitioner Wisdom & Edge Cases (Reddit / HN / Stack Overflow Synthesis)

**Leading with the technology.** "Built with retrieval and a vector database" means little to a joinery owner. Lead with the result headline and mention the technology underneath, for the technical readers who want it.

**Measuring nothing during the project.** The before number is only available before you change anything. Record it at the discovery call, Module 11.9, or in the first week, even roughly: "about three hours per quote, by Priya's estimate" is far better than nothing, provided the source is stated.

**Fragile demos.** Hosted demos on free tiers expire, API keys are revoked, and the demo that worked at handover fails six months later. Record a short screen video as a fallback, and link to it beside the live demo, so a broken host does not break the piece.

**Demos that use real client data.** Never. Use synthetic or anonymised data, and get the client's written permission before naming them at all; some clients are happy to be named but not to show their documents.

**Inflated or invented numbers.** A prospect who calls your reference and hears a different number will not call you again. Report what was measured, how, and over what period, and round down rather than up.

**Testimonials without attribution.** "Great work!" from an unnamed client persuades nobody and may breach advertising rules. A name, a role, a business, and a specific outcome make a testimonial credible.

**Too many pieces.** Five strong pieces beat fifteen uneven ones. A weak piece drags the average down, because prospects judge by the worst thing they notice.

**Personal and practice projects.** These are fine early on, Module 11.5, if labelled honestly, "a demonstration built with sample data", and they still need a problem, a measured result on the sample, and a working demo.

**Never retiring old pieces.** A piece built on a tool or model that has since been replaced suggests stale skills. Either update it with a note, "rebuilt in 2026 on a current model, same result in half the cost", or retire it.

**Ignoring mobile.** Many prospects open portfolio links on a phone between other tasks. Check that headlines, screenshots, and demo videos work on a small screen.

**Scoring as a substitute for judgement.** The rank score is a default; a weaker piece in the prospect's own industry can still be the right one to lead with.

**Hiding pricing context.** A piece that says what the client paid, or the range, helps prospects self-qualify, though only with the client's permission.

**Question 2.** Why does `proof_score` count distinct kinds of evidence, and why is a metric weighted more than a screenshot?

<details>
<summary>View Answer</summary>

Counting distinct kinds stops volume from masquerading as strength: ten screenshots show the same thing ten times, whereas a screenshot plus a measured result plus a named testimonial corroborate each other from different angles. A prospect can dismiss any one kind, a screenshot can be staged, a testimonial can be a friend, a metric can be cherry-picked, but it is much harder to dismiss three independent kinds together. Metrics weigh most, 3, because a before and after measurement answers the prospect's real question, "what will this do for me?", in comparable units; testimonials and demos weigh 2 because they add an independent voice or let the prospect see it working; code and screenshots weigh 1 because they show existence rather than value. The weights are a default that Sam can adjust for a technical buyer, who may value code more.

</details>

**Question 3.** Sam runs `maintenance` and gets eight actions. How should they be prioritised, and what prevents the portfolio decaying again?

<details>
<summary>View Answer</summary>

Prioritise by impact on the pieces most likely to be shown: the invoice workflow already has a measured result, so three small actions lift it from 3 to 6 on the checklist and make it a strong second piece; the chatbot needs a problem, a measurement, a working demo, a quote, and more evidence, which may mean redoing the project or retiring it. Decay is prevented by the fixed review cycle, the next on 15 February 2027, by recording a demo video as a fallback for live links, and by the habit of capturing the before number, a quote, and screenshots at the end of every project, so new pieces start complete rather than needing repair later.
