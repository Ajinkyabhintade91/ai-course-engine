# 10 AI Automation Workflow Ideas

Unit 7 taught how to build automations; Module 13.1 offered agent projects. This module is a set of ten **workflow** ideas: fixed sequences triggered by an event, with one or two AI steps among ordinary ones, across the main business functions. Workflows are where most small businesses get their first real value from AI, because they are predictable, cheap to run, and easy to measure. For each idea you get the trigger, the steps, and the kinds of tools involved; then the module shows how to check a design for safety gaps, estimate its value and running cost, and rank candidates so you build the most valuable one first.

Values and costs in the examples are illustrative; per-call AI costs and platform fees vary by provider and plan, so use your own.

## 1. First-Principles Mental Model & Intuition

### Jigs in the workshop

A joiner who cuts the same tenon fifty times builds a **jig**: a fixture that holds the work and guides the tool so every cut is identical and quick. Jigs do not think; they make a repeated job reliable. A workflow is a jig for office work: when a certain thing happens, do these steps in this order. An AI step is a jig with a little judgement in it, such as sorting an email or drafting a reply; and like any judgement it should be checked before anything leaves the workshop.

```text
 TRIGGER (event)  ->  STEP  ->  AI STEP  ->  CHECK  ->  HUMAN REVIEW  ->  ACTION (external)
 new email            fetch     classify     category    approve draft     send / create record
 invoice arrives      read PDF  extract      totals add  (optional)        draft accounting entry
 quote unanswered     look up   draft        -           approve           send follow-up

 rules: every AI output is checked (validation or review); every external send or post has human review first
 value = volume x minutes saved x staff cost x weeks + errors avoided
 cost  = volume x AI calls x cost per call x weeks + platform fee x 12
 rank by (value - cost) / build days
```

### Why workflows before agents

Workflows do the same thing every time, so their behaviour is predictable and their failures are visible. Anthropic's guidance on building agents describes the trade-off: workflows offer predictability for well-defined tasks; agents offer flexibility when steps cannot be known in advance, at the cost of reliability and expense. Most of the ideas below need no agent at all.

## 2. Core Theory, Mathematical Foundations & Architecture Internals

### Ten workflow ideas

| # | Idea | Function | Trigger | Steps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Enquiry triage and reply draft | Sales | New email to the enquiries inbox | Classify, validate, draft reply, human review, create CRM record |
| 2 | Quote follow-up chaser | Sales | Quote unanswered for three days | Draft follow-up, human review, send |
| 3 | Content repurposing | Marketing | New blog post published | Summarise, draft social posts in house style, review, schedule |
| 4 | Review response drafts | Marketing | New online review | Summarise, draft response, review, post |
| 5 | Invoice data capture | Operations | Invoice PDF arrives | Extract fields, validate totals, draft accounting entry |
| 6 | Supplier price watch | Operations | Supplier price list updated | Compare with previous list, summarise changes, alert |
| 7 | Support ticket routing | Support | New ticket | Categorise, suggest answer from knowledge base, route to person |
| 8 | New starter onboarding | People | New hire added to the HR system | Create checklist, draft welcome pack, request accounts, remind manager |
| 9 | Weekly cash summary | Finance | Every Monday | Read bank export, categorise, summarise, email owner |
| 10 | Meeting actions | Management | Meeting recording available | Transcribe, extract decisions and actions, create tasks, review |

Tools fall into a few kinds: a **trigger** source, such as email, a form, a schedule, or a webhook; a **workflow platform**, such as n8n or Zapier, Unit 7; an **AI step** calling a model API or a local model, Unit 10; **business systems** such as a CRM, accounting software, or a task manager; and **notification** channels.

### Design rules

Two rules catch most design mistakes:

1. **Every AI output is checked.** A validation step, such as confirming invoice totals add up or a category is one of the allowed values, or a human review, must follow AI output before anything depends on it, Module 8.5.
2. **Every external action has human review first.** Sending email, posting publicly, or changing records outside the business should not happen on AI output alone, at least until the workflow has a long, measured track record, Module 8.11.

Workflows that read untrusted text, such as emails, reviews, or tickets, are also exposed to prompt injection, OWASP's top risk for LLM applications; checks and review steps limit the damage.

### Estimating value

The value of a workflow is the staff time it saves plus the errors it avoids:

$$V = v \times \frac{m}{60} \times h \times W + E$$

with $v$ items a week, $m$ minutes saved per item, staff cost $h$ an hour, $W$ working weeks, and $E$ the yearly value of errors avoided. Forty enquiries a week, saving six minutes each at GBP 30 an hour over 48 weeks, is GBP 5,760 a year.

### Estimating running cost

Running cost is the AI calls plus the platform:

$$C = v \times c \times p \times W + 12\,f$$

with $c$ AI calls per item, $p$ cost per call, and $f$ the monthly platform fee. At an illustrative one penny per call, the enquiry workflow's two calls per item cost about GBP 38 a year in model calls, plus GBP 240 for a GBP 20 platform plan.

### Ranking candidates

With several candidates, rank them by net value per day of build effort, the same idea as Module 11.3's prioritisation:

$$\text{priority} = \frac{V - C}{\text{build days}}$$

A one-day workflow with modest value can outrank a larger one that takes days to build, and it delivers results sooner, which builds the client's confidence for the bigger projects.

### Writing the spec

Before building, write a one-page specification: the trigger, the numbered steps with their kind, AI step, check, review, or action, the tools, and the measure of success. The spec is what the client approves, Module 11.8, and what the handover documents describe, Module 11.11.

### Adapting an idea to a client

The ten ideas are templates. To adapt one, start from the client's process map, Module 11.3: find the step where the idea applies, confirm the trigger exists in their systems, for example whether quotes are tracked anywhere that can show "unanswered for three days", and replace the generic steps with their tools and wording. Then measure the baseline before building: how many items a week, how long each takes, how many go wrong. A workflow that saves six minutes on forty items a week is worth building; the same workflow on four items a week probably is not, however elegant.

### Complexity

Checking a design is linear in its steps; value, cost, and spec are linear in inputs; ranking sorts the candidates.

## 3. Production-Grade Implementation & Annotated Walkthrough

First, checking designs.

```python
WORKFLOWS = [
    {"name": "Enquiry triage and reply draft", "trigger": "new email to enquiries inbox",
     "steps": [("classify enquiry", "ai"), ("validate category", "check"), ("draft reply", "ai"),
               ("human review", "review"), ("create CRM record", "action")],
     "volume": 40, "minutes": 6, "error_saving": 0, "calls": 2, "build_days": 3},
    {"name": "Quote follow-up chaser", "trigger": "quote unanswered for 3 days",
     "steps": [("draft follow-up", "ai"), ("human review", "review"), ("send email", "action")],
     "volume": 20, "minutes": 5, "error_saving": 0, "calls": 1, "build_days": 1},
    {"name": "Invoice data capture", "trigger": "invoice PDF arrives",
     "steps": [("extract fields", "ai"), ("validate totals", "check"), ("draft accounting entry", "action")],
     "volume": 30, "minutes": 4, "error_saving": 1500, "calls": 1, "build_days": 4},
    {"name": "Review response drafts", "trigger": "new online review",
     "steps": [("summarise review", "ai"), ("draft response", "ai"), ("post response", "action")],
     "volume": 5, "minutes": 10, "error_saving": 0, "calls": 2, "build_days": 2},
]

def issues(w):
    out = []
    if not w.get("trigger"):
        out.append("no trigger")
    kinds = [k for _, k in w["steps"]]
    for i, (name, k) in enumerate(w["steps"]):
        if k == "action" and any(x in name for x in ("send", "post")) and "review" not in kinds[:i]:
            out.append(f"external action '{name}' without human review")
    if "ai" in kinds and not ("check" in kinds or "review" in kinds):
        out.append("AI output never checked")
    return out

for w in WORKFLOWS:
    print(w["name"], issues(w))
```

- **Three designs pass.** Each AI output is checked or reviewed, and the chaser reviews before sending.
- **The review responder posts publicly with no check at all:** two issues. Add a human review before posting.

Next, annual value.

```python
def annual_value(volume, minutes, hourly, error_saving=0, weeks=48):
    return round(volume * minutes / 60 * hourly * weeks + error_saving, 2)

print(annual_value(40, 6, 30), annual_value(30, 4, 30, 1500))
```

- **Enquiry triage: GBP 5,760 a year.**
- **Invoice capture: GBP 2,880 of time plus GBP 1,500 of errors avoided, GBP 4,380.**

Then, annual running cost.

```python
def annual_cost(volume, calls, cost_per_call, platform_monthly, weeks=48):
    return round(volume * calls * cost_per_call * weeks + platform_monthly * 12, 2)

print(annual_cost(40, 2, 0.01, 20), annual_cost(40, 2, 0.01, 0))
```

- **GBP 278.40 a year, of which only GBP 38.40 is model calls;** the platform plan dominates at this volume.
- **Running a local model, Unit 10, or a free platform tier changes the picture,** but adds setup and maintenance.

Then, ranking.

```python
def rank(ws, hourly=30, cost_per_call=0.01, platform_monthly=20):
    rows = []
    for w in ws:
        net = annual_value(w["volume"], w["minutes"], hourly, w["error_saving"]) \
              - annual_cost(w["volume"], w["calls"], cost_per_call, platform_monthly)
        rows.append((w["name"], round(net, 2), round(net / w["build_days"], 2)))
    return sorted(rows, key=lambda r: -r[2])

for row in rank(WORKFLOWS):
    print(row)
```

- **The quote chaser ranks first, GBP 2,150 net per build day,** because it takes one day.
- **Enquiry triage has the largest net value, GBP 5,482, but takes three days,** so it ranks second.
- **The review responder ranks last,** low volume and, as designed, unsafe.

Finally, the one-page spec.

```python
def spec(w):
    lines = [f"WORKFLOW: {w['name']}", f"WHEN: {w['trigger']}"]
    lines += [f"{i}. {name} [{kind}]" for i, (name, kind) in enumerate(w["steps"], 1)]
    lines.append(f"MEASURE: {w['minutes']} minutes saved per item, about {w['volume']} items a week")
    return lines

for line in spec(WORKFLOWS[1]):
    print(line)
```

- **Trigger, numbered steps with their kind, and the success measure.**
- **The client approves this page before building,** and it becomes the handover document's first page.

**Complexity.** Linear apart from sorting.

## 4. Practitioner Wisdom & Edge Cases (Reddit / HN / Stack Overflow Synthesis)

**Unchecked AI output.** Add validation or review.

**Auto-sending to customers.** Review first.

**Automating rare tasks.** Low volume, low value. Count first.

**Ignoring platform fees.** At low volume they dominate cost.

**Biggest first.** Quick wins build trust.

**No error handling.** Failed steps must alert someone, Unit 7.

**No spec.** Clients approve pages, not ideas.

**Prompt injection from emails and reviews.** Treat inputs as untrusted.

**Agent where a workflow will do.** Prefer the predictable option.

**Unmeasured results.** Track minutes saved after launch.

**Forgetting maintenance.** Prompts and integrations need care, Module 11.12.

**Copying generic templates.** Adapt to the client's process, Module 11.3.

**Question 1.** A client's draft workflow summarises new online reviews, drafts a response, and posts it automatically. Using `issues`, explain what is wrong and how to fix it.

<details>
<summary>View Answer</summary>

`issues` reports two problems: the external action "post response" happens without any human review before it, and the AI output is never checked at all, since the design has no validation or review step. Posting AI-written responses publicly without review risks inaccurate, inappropriate, or manipulated replies; reviews are untrusted text that could even contain instructions aimed at the model, the prompt-injection risk. The fix is to insert a human review step between drafting and posting, so the owner approves or edits each response, and optionally a check that the draft avoids specific claims, such as refunds, that need a manager. With five reviews a week, review takes minutes. After that change the workflow passes both design rules, though `rank` still puts it last because of its low volume.

</details>

**Question 2.** Using `annual_value` and `annual_cost`, explain why the enquiry workflow's running cost is dominated by the platform fee, and what that implies.

<details>
<summary>View Answer</summary>

`annual_value(40, 6, 30)` puts the enquiry workflow's value at GBP 5,760 a year. `annual_cost(40, 2, 0.01, 20)` gives GBP 278.40 a year, of which the model calls are only 40 items times 2 calls times one penny times 48 weeks, GBP 38.40, and the platform plan is GBP 240. At small-business volumes, model calls are cheap and fixed fees dominate. Two implications follow: it is worth combining several workflows on one platform plan, because each extra workflow adds value without adding much cost; and switching to a local model, Unit 10, saves very little here while adding setup and maintenance, so it should be chosen for privacy, Module 10.13, rather than cost. Per-call prices vary by model, so the estimate should be redone with the client's actual provider.

<details>
<summary>View Answer</summary>

`rank` scores each workflow by net annual value divided by build days. Enquiry triage has the largest net value, about GBP 5,482 a year, but takes three days, about GBP 1,827 per build day; the quote chaser has a smaller net value, about GBP 2,150, but takes one day, so it scores about GBP 2,150 per build day and ranks first. Building it first delivers a working result within a day, shows the client real value quickly, and builds the trust that makes the larger triage project an easy decision afterwards; it also tackles a speed problem, slow follow-up, that Module 11.3 found loses jobs. The ranking is a guide rather than a rule: if the client's biggest pain is clearly the enquiry backlog, Sam might start there, but the arithmetic makes the trade-off explicit.
