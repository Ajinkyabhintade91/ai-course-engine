# The 30-day plan

Module 12.1 explained how the roadmap works: daily learning, building, and review, weekly projects, tracking, and adapting to your level. This module gives the plan itself, day by day. It has four weeks, each ending in a deliverable you can show to someone: a tested prompt library, a working automation, an agent or small application, and a portfolio page with an offer. The last two days send your first outreach and plan what comes next. Each day names a theme, the area of the course it belongs to, and the unit or module to use.

The plan assumes about seven hours a week, Module 12.1's example; adjust it with the weekly review. Dates in the lab use a start of 17 November 2026 for illustration.

## 1. First-Principles Mental Model & Intuition

### Four pieces of furniture in four weeks

An apprentice's first month might be: a set of joints on a practice board in week one, a small box in week two, a stool in week three, and in week four a cabinet good enough to put in the shop window with a price tag. Each piece uses everything learned before and adds something new. The roadmap is built the same way: each week's deliverable uses the previous weeks' skills, and the last week turns them into something you can sell.

```text
 WEEK 1  days 1-7    FOUNDATIONS AND PROMPTING        deliverable: a tested prompt library for your niche
 WEEK 2  days 8-14   TOOLS AND AUTOMATION             deliverable: a working, documented automation
 WEEK 3  days 15-21  AGENTS AND CODING                deliverable: an agent or small app, evaluated, deployed or local
 WEEK 4  days 22-28  PORTFOLIO, OFFER, OUTREACH       deliverable: portfolio page + package + price
         days 29-30  send outreach; 30-day review and next plan (Module 12.3)

 every 7th day: review + deliverable      each day: learn (module) -> build (project) -> review (recall)
```

### One project thread

The strongest results come when the weekly projects form **one thread** around a single niche problem, such as quoting for joinery workshops: week 1's prompts price materials, week 2's automation runs them on incoming enquiries, week 3's agent adds tool calling, retrieval, and evaluation, and week 4 presents the result as a case study and a package. By day 30 you have one coherent piece of work and an offer built on it, not four unrelated experiments.

### Scheduling

With a start date $d_0$, day $n$ falls on $d_0 + (n - 1)$ days, and its week is

$$w(n) = \min\left(\left\lfloor \frac{n - 1}{7} \right\rfloor + 1,\ 4\right)$$

so days 29 and 30 belong to week 4. Starting on Tuesday 17 November 2026, the weekly reviews fall on 23 November, 30 November, 7 December, and 14 December, and the final review on 16 December.

### Adapting the plan

Module 12.1's placement marks areas to skim. In the plan, a day whose area is skimmed becomes **extra practice** in a focus area, rotating through the focus areas in turn, so the time moves to where it is needed without changing the weekly structure. Review days are never replaced.

### Progress

At any date, the days that should be done are those up to and including today. Progress is the share of all thirty days completed, and **days behind** is the number of due days not yet completed:

$$\text{behind} = \left|\{n \le n_{\text{today}}\}\right| - \left|\{n \le n_{\text{today}} : n \text{ done}\}\right|$$

Being one or two days behind is normal and recovered at the weekly review; more than that means the load should drop, Module 12.1.

### Complexity

Scheduling, look-ups, deliverables, adaptation, and progress are all linear in the thirty days.

def adapt(sched, skip, focus):
    out, k = [], 0
    for n, d, w, t, a, m in sched:
        if a in skip:
            target = focus[k % len(focus)] if focus else "review"
            k += 1
            out.append((n, d, w, f"Extra practice: {target}", target, "project extension"))
        else:
            out.append((n, d, w, t, a, m))
    return out

- **Review days and the deliverables stay;** the structure of the weeks is unchanged.

Finally, progress.

```python
def progress(sched, done_days, today):
    t = date.fromisoformat(today)
    due = [r[0] for r in sched if date.fromisoformat(r[1]) <= t]
    behind = len(due) - len([n for n in due if n in done_days])
    return round(len(set(done_days)) / len(sched) * 100), behind, behind <= 2

print(progress(S, list(range(1, 8)), "2026-11-24"))
print(progress(S, [1, 2, 3, 4, 5], "2026-11-24"))
```

- **On 24 November with days 1 to 7 done: 23 per cent complete, one day behind, on track.**
- **With only days 1 to 5 done: three days behind,** a signal to reduce the load at the next review.

## 4. Practitioner Wisdom & Edge Cases (Reddit / HN / Stack Overflow Synthesis)

**Unrelated weekly projects.** Keep one thread around one niche problem.

**Skipping deliverables.** Each week ends with something shown to someone.

**Perfecting week 1.** Good enough is enough; the thread improves it later.

**Agents before workflows.** Simple automation first, Unit 7, then agents.

**No evaluation set.** Without day 17 you cannot tell if the agent works.

**Deploying nothing.** A local or deployed app beats a notebook.

**Leaving business to the end and skipping it.** Week 4 is where earning starts.

**Waiting to be "ready" before outreach.** Day 29 is the deadline.

**Falling behind silently.** Check progress daily; adjust weekly.

**Replacing review days.** They are never skipped.

**Starting on a busy week.** Pick a start date you can protect.

**Treating the plan as rigid.** Adapt it to your level and life.
