# DeepSeek

**DeepSeek**, an AI company based in Hangzhou and funded by the quantitative hedge fund High-Flyer, became world news in January 2025 when its **R1** reasoning model, released with open weights under the MIT licence, matched leading closed models on many reasoning benchmarks at a fraction of their reported training cost. For local AI, DeepSeek matters in three ways: its architectural ideas, such as multi-head latent attention and fine-grained mixtures of experts, now appear across the field; its **distilled** R1 models are among the most downloaded on Ollama; and its models raise the clearest questions about **trust and deployment**, where the difference between using a hosted app and running weights locally is large.

Facts are from DeepSeek's papers, model cards, and change log, Ollama's library, NIST, and news reporting cited at the end, at the time of writing (November 2026).

## 1. First-Principles Mental Model & Intuition

### A master craftsman and the apprentices

Think of the full DeepSeek-R1 as a **master craftsman**: enormously skilled, but needing a whole workshop, around 400 GB of memory at 4 bits. The **distilled** models are **apprentices** trained by watching the master work: other companies' models, Qwen and Llama, fine-tuned on R1's reasoning traces. An apprentice works on a laptop and has picked up the master's habit of thinking aloud, but is not the master. When someone says "I'm running DeepSeek R1 on my laptop", they almost certainly mean an apprentice, and the distinction matters for quality, for the licence, which comes partly from the apprentice's original employer, and for expectations.

```text
                           DeepSeek-R1 (671B total, 37B active, MoE, MIT)
                                    | reasoning traces
            +-------------+---------+----------+-------------+--------------+
            v             v                    v             v              v
   Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B  Qwen2.5-Math-7B   Qwen3-8B (R1-0528)  Qwen2.5-14B/32B  Llama-3.3-70B
   deepseek-r1:1.5b   deepseek-r1:7b    deepseek-r1:8b      :14b / :32b      deepseek-r1:70b
   (MIT + Apache 2.0 from the base)                                          (MIT + Llama 3.3 licence)

 deepseek-r1:671b = the real R1, 404 GB at 4 bits
 V3 (Dec 2024) -> R1 (Jan 2025) -> V3.1 hybrid thinking -> V3.2 sparse attention -> V4 (2026, 1M context)
```

### Three ways to "use DeepSeek"

The **hosted app** and website run on DeepSeek's servers in China. The **API** also runs there. **Local weights** run on your machine and send nothing anywhere. These are very different propositions for privacy, regulation, and policy, and conflating them is the most common mistake in discussions of DeepSeek.

## 2. Core Theory, Mathematical Foundations & Architecture Internals

### Lineage

- **DeepSeek-V2** (2024): a 236B-parameter mixture of experts with 21B active, introducing **multi-head latent attention**.
- **DeepSeek-V3** (December 2024): 671B total, 37B active, trained on 14.8 trillion tokens, 128K context.
- **DeepSeek-R1** (January 2025): reasoning trained largely with reinforcement learning on top of V3; updated as **R1-0528** in May 2025.
- **V3.1** (August 2025): one model with thinking and non-thinking modes. **V3.2-Exp** (September 2025) and **V3.2** (December 2025): **DeepSeek Sparse Attention** for cheaper long contexts.
- **V4** (preview April 2026): V4-Pro, 1.6T total and 49B active, and V4-Flash, 284B total and 13B active, both with 1M-token context; later updates added V4.1-Flash with native image input.

On Ollama, the V4 models are offered as **cloud** tags, which run on Ollama's service rather than locally, Module 10.2.

### Key ideas

**Multi-head latent attention (MLA)** compresses the keys and values into a small latent vector per token, so the KV cache, Module 10.10, is far smaller than standard attention's; the V2 paper reports a 93.3 per cent reduction. **DeepSeekMoE** uses many fine-grained experts plus always-active shared experts. The V3 report adds **multi-token prediction**, auxiliary-loss-free load balancing across experts, and **FP8** mixed-precision training. **GRPO**, group relative policy optimisation, introduced in the DeepSeekMath paper, is the reinforcement learning method used for R1: for each prompt the model samples a group of answers, scores them with rules such as "is the maths answer correct", and updates towards answers that beat the group average:

$$A_i = \frac{r_i - \operatorname{mean}(r_1, \dots, r_G)}{\operatorname{std}(r_1, \dots, r_G)}$$

which avoids training a separate value model. The R1 paper reports that reasoning behaviours such as self-checking emerged from this training.

### The distilled models

The R1 card lists six distillations, fine-tuned on samples generated by R1: Qwen2.5-Math 1.5B and 7B, Qwen2.5 14B and 32B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct. A later distillation put R1-0528's reasoning into Qwen3-8B. On Ollama, `deepseek-r1:1.5b` to `:70b` are these distillations, and the default `deepseek-r1:latest`, at 8B and 5.2 GB, is the Qwen3-8B one; only `deepseek-r1:671b`, 404 GB, is the full model.

| `7b` | 4.7 GB | Qwen2.5-Math-7B distill | MIT and Apache 2.0 |

The card says the distillations keep their base models' licences, so the 70B inherits Llama's conditions, Module 10.6.

### Licences

R1, R1-0528, V3.2, and V4-Pro are released under the **MIT** licence for code and weights, per their cards. The original V3 used MIT for code with a separate DeepSeek model licence for the weights, which permits commercial use with use-based restrictions. MIT is short and permissive: keep the copyright and licence notice.

### Usage advice from the R1 card

The R1 card recommends a temperature between 0.5 and 0.7, with 0.6 recommended, to prevent endless repetition or incoherent output; avoiding a **system prompt**, with all instructions in the user message; and, for maths, asking the model to reason step by step and put the final answer in `\boxed{}`. The R1-0528 generation added system prompt support. R1 writes its reasoning between `<think>` and `</think>` before the answer; Ollama separates it for supported models, but raw outputs must be split by hand, and some chat templates insert the opening tag themselves, so a response may contain only the closing tag.

### Running the full model locally

The full R1 is a mixture of experts, so Module 10.10's rule applies: memory for all 671B parameters, speed from the 37B active:

$$\text{read per token} \approx 404 \text{ GB} \times \frac{37}{671} \approx 22.3 \text{ GB}$$

A 4-bit file of 404 GB needs a machine with roughly 450 GB of usable memory. People run it on large-memory Macs, with the GPU memory limit raised, and on servers with many memory channels. A 12-channel DDR5-4800 server has about $12 \times 4.8 \times 10^9 \times 8 \approx 460$ GB/s of bandwidth, enough for about 12 tokens per second at 60 per cent efficiency; a gaming PC cannot hold it at all.

### Trust, policy, and deployment

**Hosted app and API.** In early 2025 several governments acted against DeepSeek's hosted app: Italy's regulator blocked it, and Taiwan, Australia, and a number of US agencies and states restricted it on government devices, citing data protection and security. These concerns are about data sent to DeepSeek's servers.

**Model behaviour.** The US National Institute of Standards and Technology's Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) published an evaluation of DeepSeek models in 2025. Press coverage of the report described it as finding that the models lagged leading US models, were more susceptible to jailbreaking and agent hijacking, and more often echoed Chinese Communist Party narratives. These properties are trained into the weights, so they come with local use too.

**Local weights.** Running weights locally sends no data to DeepSeek, which removes the data-protection concern, but it does not remove the trained-in behaviour. A sensible policy therefore distinguishes **where data goes** from **how the model behaves**, and tests the model on the organisation's own sensitive cases, Module 10.15, as it would any model.

### Complexity

Resolving a tag and building a request are constant time; splitting output is linear in its length; the hardware estimate is linear in machines; the risk profile is constant per mode.

## 3. Production-Grade Implementation & Annotated Walkthrough

First, what a tag really is.

```python
R1_TAGS = {
    "1.5b": ("Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B", "apache-2.0", 1.1), "7b": ("Qwen2.5-Math-7B", "apache-2.0", 4.7),
    "8b": ("Qwen3-8B", "apache-2.0", 5.2), "14b": ("Qwen2.5-14B", "apache-2.0", 9.0),
    "32b": ("Qwen2.5-32B", "apache-2.0", 20.0), "70b": ("Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct", "llama3.3", 43.0),
    "671b": ("DeepSeek-R1", "mit", 404.0),
}

def resolve_r1(ref):
    model, _, tag = ref.partition(":")
    tag = tag or "latest"
    if tag == "latest":
        tag = "8b"
    base, base_licence, gb = R1_TAGS[tag]
    full = tag == "671b"
    licences = ["mit"] if full else ["mit", base_licence]
    return {"tag": tag, "full_r1": full, "base": base, "licences": licences, "gb": gb}

for ref in ["deepseek-r1", "deepseek-r1:70b", "deepseek-r1:671b"]:
    print(ref, resolve_r1(ref))
```

- **The default is the 8B Qwen3 distillation.** Not R1 itself.
- **The 70B carries the Llama 3.3 licence as well as MIT.** Its attribution and naming rules apply, Module 10.6.
- **Only `671b` is the full model, at 404 GB.**

Next, separating thinking from the answer.

```python
def strip_think(text):
    if "</think>" not in text:
        if "<think>" in text:
            return text.split("<think>", 1)[1].strip(), ""
        return "", text.strip()
    before, _, after = text.partition("</think>")
    return before.replace("<think>", "", 1).strip(), after.strip()

print(strip_think("<think>Check SAW-3 blade size.</think>The 254 mm blade fits."))
print(strip_think("Check size.</think>It fits."), strip_think("<think>unfinished"))
```

- **A normal response splits cleanly.**
- **A missing opening tag is handled.** Some templates insert `<think>` in the prompt, so the response starts mid-thought.
- **An unfinished thought means no answer.** Treat it as incomplete.

Then, building requests the way the card advises.

```python
def r1_messages(system, prompt, version="r1", maths=False):
    if maths:
        prompt += "\nPlease reason step by step, and put your final answer within \\boxed{}."
    if version == "r1":
        content = (system + "\n\n" + prompt) if system else prompt
        msgs = [{"role": "user", "content": content}]
    else:
        msgs = ([{"role": "system", "content": system}] if system else []) + [{"role": "user", "content": prompt}]
    return msgs, {"temperature": 0.6, "top_p": 0.95}

print(r1_messages("You are the shop assistant.", "Is 254 mm right for SAW-3?"))
print(r1_messages("You are the shop assistant.", "Total for 3 blades at GBP 12 each?", "r1-0528", True))
```

- **For the original R1, the system prompt is folded into the user message.**
- **For R1-0528 and its distillation, a system message is allowed.**
- **Maths prompts get the card's step-by-step and boxed-answer instruction,** and every request uses temperature 0.6.

Then, what the full model needs.

```python
def moe_local(total_b, active_b, file_gb, machines, headroom=1.1, efficiency=0.6):
    read = file_gb * active_b / total_b
    out = []
    for name, mem, bw in machines:
        fits = file_gb * headroom <= mem
        out.append((name, fits, round(efficiency * bw / read, 1) if fits else 0.0))
    return round(read, 2), out

MACHINES = [("RTX 4090 PC, 24 GB VRAM + 64 GB RAM", 88, 1008),
            ("Mac Studio 512 GB, GPU limit raised to 480 GB", 480, 819),
            ("Server, 768 GB DDR5-4800, 12 channels", 768, 460),
            ("Workstation, 256 GB DDR5", 256, 90)]
print(moe_local(671, 37, 404, MACHINES))
```

- **22.28 GB is read per token.** Memory must hold all 404 GB plus headroom.
- **The large Mac and the server run it, at about 22 and 12 tokens per second.** The gaming PC and the 256 GB workstation cannot hold it.
- **Headroom is smaller here, 10 per cent,** because MLA keeps the KV cache small; long contexts still need more.

Finally, a deployment risk profile.

```python
MODES = {
    "app":       {"data_leaves_machine": True,  "processed_by_deepseek": True,  "trained_behaviour": True, "app_bans_apply": True},
    "api":       {"data_leaves_machine": True,  "processed_by_deepseek": True,  "trained_behaviour": True, "app_bans_apply": False},
    "cloud-tag": {"data_leaves_machine": True,  "processed_by_deepseek": False, "trained_behaviour": True, "app_bans_apply": False},
    "local":     {"data_leaves_machine": False, "processed_by_deepseek": False, "trained_behaviour": True, "app_bans_apply": False},
}

def risk_profile(mode, policy):
    flags = MODES[mode]
    issues = []
    if policy.get("data_must_stay_local") and flags["data_leaves_machine"]:
        issues.append("data leaves the machine")
    if policy.get("no_chinese_hosting") and flags["processed_by_deepseek"]:
        issues.append("processed on DeepSeek's service")
    if policy.get("government_device") and flags["app_bans_apply"]:
        issues.append("hosted app restricted on government devices in several jurisdictions")
    if flags["trained_behaviour"] and policy.get("sensitive_topics"):
        issues.append("evaluate trained-in behaviour on sensitive topics")
    return issues

POLICY = {"data_must_stay_local": True, "no_chinese_hosting": True, "government_device": True, "sensitive_topics": True}
for mode in MODES:
    print(mode, risk_profile(mode, POLICY))
```

- **The app raises four issues, the API three, an Ollama cloud tag two, and local weights one.**
- **Local weights still raise the behaviour issue.** Running locally fixes where data goes, not what the model learned.
- **A cloud tag runs on Ollama's service, not DeepSeek's,** but data still leaves the machine.

**Complexity.** Constant or linear throughout.

## 4. Practitioner Wisdom & Edge Cases (Reddit / HN / Stack Overflow Synthesis)

**"I'm running R1 on my laptop."** It is a distillation. Say which one.

**Comparing a distill with the full model's benchmarks.** They differ widely. Test the one you run.

**Forgetting inherited licences.** The 70B carries Llama's terms.

**System prompts with the original R1.** The card advises against them. Put instructions in the user message.

**Temperature too low or too high.** The card recommends 0.6. Repetition and incoherence follow otherwise.

**Thinking shown to customers.** It is long and unpolished. Hide it.

**Missing opening `<think>`.** Templates may insert it. Split on the closing tag.

**Assuming local equals safe.** Data stays local; behaviour does not change. Evaluate.

**Assuming an Ollama cloud tag is local.** It is not. Check for `:cloud`.

**Running the full model on too little memory.** It will swap and crawl. Budget 450 GB or more at 4 bits.

**Thinking length.** Reasoning models can spend thousands of tokens. Budget context and time.

**Policy confusion.** Bans target the hosted app on certain devices. Read the policy that applies to you.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 1
def resolve_r1(ref):
    """Which model a deepseek-r1 tag really is, and the licences it carries."""
    model, _, tag = ref.partition(":")
    tag = tag or "latest"
    if tag == "latest":
        tag = "8b"
    base, base_licence, gb = R1_TAGS[tag]
    full = tag == "671b"
    licences = ["mit"] if full else ["mit", base_licence]
    return {"tag": tag, "full_r1": full, "base": base, "licences": licences, "gb": gb}


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 2
def strip_think(text):
    """Separate reasoning from the answer, tolerating a template-supplied opening tag."""
    if "</think>" not in text:
        if "<think>" in text:
            return text.split("<think>", 1)[1].strip(), ""
        return "", text.strip()
    before, _, after = text.partition("</think>")
    return before.replace("<think>", "", 1).strip(), after.strip()


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 3
def r1_messages(system, prompt, version="r1", maths=False):
    """Requests that follow the R1 model card's usage recommendations."""
    if maths:
        prompt += "\nPlease reason step by step, and put your final answer within \\boxed{}."
    if version == "r1":
        content = (system + "\n\n" + prompt) if system else prompt
        msgs = [{"role": "user", "content": content}]
    else:
        msgs = ([{"role": "system", "content": system}] if system else []) + [{"role": "user", "content": prompt}]
    return msgs, {"temperature": 0.6, "top_p": 0.95}


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 4
def moe_local(total_b, active_b, file_gb, machines, headroom=1.1, efficiency=0.6):
    """Memory for every expert; speed from the active share read per token."""
    read = file_gb * active_b / total_b
    out = []
    for name, mem, bw in machines:
        fits = file_gb * headroom <= mem
        out.append((name, fits, round(efficiency * bw / read, 1) if fits else 0.0))
    return round(read, 2), out


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 5
def risk_profile(mode, policy):
    """Where data goes and how the model behaves, as separate questions."""
    flags = MODES[mode]
    issues = []
    if policy.get("data_must_stay_local") and flags["data_leaves_machine"]:
        issues.append("data leaves the machine")
    if policy.get("no_chinese_hosting") and flags["processed_by_deepseek"]:
        issues.append("processed on DeepSeek's service")
    if policy.get("government_device") and flags["app_bans_apply"]:
        issues.append("hosted app restricted on government devices in several jurisdictions")
    if flags["trained_behaviour"] and policy.get("sensitive_topics"):
        issues.append("evaluate trained-in behaviour on sensitive topics")
    return issues
