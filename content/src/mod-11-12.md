# Retaining Clients

Finding clients is the most expensive part of an independent business, Module 11.1. Keeping them is the cheapest growth there is: a satisfied client buys again, pays a monthly fee for support, and refers others like them. AI systems in particular need ongoing care, because models change, data changes, prices change, and the business's needs change, so a support relationship is genuinely useful rather than a sales trick. This module covers retainers, maintenance, and support plans; reporting value and return on investment each month; and upsells, referrals, and long-term relationships.

Plans, fees, and figures continue the Harper Joinery example and are illustrative.

## 1. First-Principles Mental Model & Intuition

### The joiner's maintenance round

A joiner who fitted a shop's counters and shelving does not disappear. Twice a year they come back, adjust the hinges, fix the drawer that sticks, and notice that the shop has outgrown its storage. Because they have kept in touch and done good work, they are the obvious person to build the new storage, and the shop owner mentions them to the café next door. The maintenance round costs little, keeps the work in good order, and generates most of the joiner's future business.

```text
 PROJECT (one-off) --> SUPPORT PLAN (monthly) --> VALUE REPORT (monthly) --> REVIEW (quarterly)
                        Care    GBP 150, 2 h       quotes x minutes saved     what changed, what next
                        Improve GBP 432, 8 h       + errors avoided           upsell when the client asks
                        Partner GBP 950, 16 h      value / fee >= 1.5 healthy  referral when they are happy
 churn signals: usage drops, late payments, unanswered updates, champion leaves, no value report
 lifetime value = project + monthly fee x months + referrals x project value
```

### Why AI systems need care

An AI tool is not a bookshelf: it depends on models that are updated or retired, Module 10.8, on prompts tuned to data that drifts, and on APIs and prices that change. Without monitoring, quality decays quietly. A support plan that includes monitoring, fixes, and small improvements is in the client's interest, which is why it is easy to sell honestly.

## 2. Core Theory, Mathematical Foundations & Architecture Internals

### Retainers, maintenance, and support plans

Support is usually offered as tiered monthly plans, like the delivery packages in Module 11.6:

| Plan | Fee | Hours | Includes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Care | GBP 150 | 2 | Monitoring, fixes |
| Improve | GBP 432 | 8 | + a monthly improvement and a value report |
| Partner | GBP 950 | 16 | + quarterly review and new automations |

Each plan should state what is included, the response time for problems, what happens to unused hours, and what costs are passed through, such as API usage. The economics per plan are the effective rate per included hour and the margin after running costs:

$$\text{rate} = \frac{\text{fee}}{\text{hours}}, \qquad \text{margin} = \frac{\text{fee} - \text{running costs}}{\text{fee}}$$

Clients often use fewer hours than included, so the effective rate per hour used is higher, Module 11.7.

### Reporting value and return on investment

The most important thing a support plan delivers is a **monthly value report**: a short statement of what the system did and what it was worth. For the quote assistant, value comes from minutes saved per quote and errors avoided:

$$V_{\text{month}} = n \times \frac{\Delta t}{60} \times h + E$$

with $n$ quotes in the month, $\Delta t$ minutes saved per quote, staff cost $h$ per hour, and $E$ the value of errors avoided. With 120 quotes, 13 minutes saved each, GBP 30 an hour, and GBP 432 of errors avoided, a month's value is about GBP 1,212. Comparing with the fee gives a simple health measure:

$$\text{value ratio} = \frac{V_{\text{month}}}{\text{fee}}$$

At or above about 3 the plan is clearly worth it; between 1.5 and 3, healthy; below 1.5, at risk, either because the tool is underused or because the plan is too large for the client. A falling ratio is an early warning to act on, not to hide.

### Spotting churn risk

Clients rarely leave without warning. Signals include **usage dropping**, **late payments**, **updates going unanswered**, the **champion leaving**, the person who hired you, and **no recent value report**, so the client has no reminder of the benefit. Weighting the signals gives a simple score and level:

$$\text{risk score} = \sum_{s \in \text{signals present}} w_s$$

When risk is medium or high, act: call the client, review usage together, fix what is wrong, or right-size the plan. Reducing a plan honestly keeps a client; letting them cancel in frustration loses them and their referrals.

### Upsells

The best upsells come from the client's own requests. The change log from delivery, Module 11.11, and support tickets show what the client keeps asking for; if a higher plan or a new project includes it, offer it, with its value. Offering things the client has not asked for, or that the value report does not support, damages trust.

### Referrals and long-term relationships

A satisfied client is the best source of new clients, Module 11.4. Ask for referrals at moments of visible success, such as after a strong value report or the acceptance result, Module 11.11, and make it easy: a short description of who you help and a line they can forward. Testimonials and reviews must be genuine and freely given; the FTC's 2024 rule bans buying reviews or testimonials and fake ones, Module 11.5. Reichheld's work on the "net promoter" question, how likely a customer is to recommend you, popularised the idea that willingness to refer is a strong signal of loyalty.

### Lifetime value

A client's **lifetime value** is everything they bring over the relationship:

$$\text{LTV} = \text{project} + \text{monthly fee} \times \text{months} + \text{referrals} \times \text{average referral value}$$

A GBP 2,400 project followed by 18 months of a GBP 432 plan and two referred projects of GBP 2,400 each is worth about GBP 14,976, more than six times the first project. That is why it is worth investing in retention.

### Quarterly reviews

Every three months, step back from the monthly reports for a short review with the client. Look at the trend in the value ratio, usage, and any incidents; ask what has changed in the business, such as new staff, new suppliers, busier seasons, or new services; and agree priorities for the next quarter. The review is where right-sizing happens honestly, in either direction: a plan that is too large for a quiet business is reduced before the client resents it, and requests that keep appearing in support become a proposal for the next project. Keep a short written record of each review, because it becomes the history of the relationship and the evidence behind every future recommendation.

### Complexity

Plan economics, value reports, churn scores, and lifetime value are constant time per client; finding an upsell is linear in plans and requests.

## 3. Production-Grade Implementation & Annotated Walkthrough

First, plan economics.

```python
PLANS = [{"name": "Care", "fee": 150, "hours": 2, "includes": ["monitoring", "fixes"]},
         {"name": "Improve", "fee": 432, "hours": 8, "includes": ["monitoring", "fixes", "monthly improvement", "value report"]},
         {"name": "Partner", "fee": 950, "hours": 16, "includes": ["monitoring", "fixes", "monthly improvement",
                                                                  "value report", "quarterly review", "new automations"]}]

def plan_economics(plans, run_costs):
    return [(p["name"], round(p["fee"] / p["hours"], 2), round((p["fee"] - run_costs) / p["fee"] * 100, 1)) for p in plans]

print(plan_economics(PLANS, 25))
```

- **Care pays GBP 75 per included hour; Improve GBP 54; Partner about GBP 59.**
- **Margins after GBP 25 of monthly running costs: 83 to 97 per cent.** Running costs weigh most on the smallest plan.
- **API usage above an agreed level should be passed through,** so a busy month does not erase the margin.

Next, the monthly value report.

```python
def monthly_value(items, minutes_saved, hourly_cost, error_saving=0.0):
    return round(items * minutes_saved / 60 * hourly_cost + error_saving, 2)

def value_report(value, fee):
    ratio = round(value / fee, 2)
    return ratio, ("strong" if ratio >= 3 else ("healthy" if ratio >= 1.5 else "at risk"))

v = monthly_value(120, 13, 30, 432)
print(v, value_report(v, 432))
print(value_report(monthly_value(40, 13, 30), 432))
```

- **120 quotes saving 13 minutes each, plus GBP 432 of errors avoided: about GBP 1,212 a month.**
- **Against the GBP 432 Improve fee, a ratio of 2.81: healthy.**
- **In a quiet month of 40 quotes without counting errors, the ratio is 0.6: at risk.** The report should say so and explain why.

Then, churn risk.

```python
SIGNALS = {"usage_drop": 3, "late_payment": 2, "unanswered_updates": 2, "champion_left": 3, "no_value_report": 1}

def churn_risk(flags):
    score = sum(SIGNALS[f] for f in flags if f in SIGNALS)
    return score, ("high" if score >= 5 else ("medium" if score >= 2 else "low"))

print(churn_risk([]), churn_risk(["no_value_report", "late_payment"]), churn_risk(["usage_drop", "champion_left"]))
```

- **No signals: low.** Two minor signals: medium, a prompt to send a value report and check in.
- **Usage falling after the champion left: high.** Call the new contact and re-establish the value.

Then, finding upsells from requests.

```python
def upsell(requests_log, current_plan, plans):
    names = [p["name"] for p in plans]
    i = names.index(current_plan)
    have = set(plans[i]["includes"])
    for p in plans[i + 1:]:
        new = [x for x in p["includes"] if x not in have]
        if any(r in new for r in requests_log):
            return p["name"], [r for r in requests_log if r in new]
    return None, []

print(upsell(["new automations", "fixes"], "Improve", PLANS))
print(upsell(["fixes"], "Partner", PLANS))
```

- **Priya has asked for new automations, which only Partner includes,** so Partner is the fitting upsell, with that request as the reason.
- **A client already on the top plan gets no plan upsell;** new projects are the next conversation.

Finally, lifetime value.

```python
def lifetime_value(project, monthly_fee, months, referrals, referral_value):
    return round(project + monthly_fee * months + referrals * referral_value, 2)

print(lifetime_value(2400, 432, 18, 2, 2400), lifetime_value(2400, 0, 0, 0, 0))
```

- **GBP 14,976 for a retained, referring client, against GBP 2,400 for a one-off project.**
- **Retention multiplies the value of every hour spent winning the first project.**

**Complexity.** Constant per client; linear in plans for upsells.

## 4. Practitioner Wisdom & Edge Cases (Reddit / HN / Stack Overflow Synthesis)

**Disappearing after handover.** The relationship ends by default. Offer support.

**Retainers without a value report.** Clients forget why they pay. Report monthly.

**Hiding a bad month.** Report it with the reason and the fix.

**Unlimited hours.** State what is included and what rolls over.

**Absorbing API costs.** Pass usage above a threshold through.

**Ignoring churn signals.** Act on falling usage and silence.

**Champion leaves, nobody notices.** Build relationships with more than one person.

**Pushy upsells.** Offer only what the client has asked for or the data supports.

**Never asking for referrals.** Ask at moments of success.

**Fake or paid reviews.** Use genuine ones only.

**Right-sizing seen as failure.** A smaller plan keeps a client.

**No quarterly review.** Needs change; review them.
