# Delivering Projects

Delivery is where reputation is made. Clients rarely remember the elegance of the code; they remember whether you did what you said, when you said, whether they were kept informed, whether the scope stayed under control without arguments, and whether they could use the result after you left. This module covers delivering AI projects with milestones, demos, and feedback loops; managing scope creep with change requests; and finishing well with documentation, handover, training, and an acceptance test against the success measure agreed at kick-off, Module 11.10.

Dates and figures continue the Harper Joinery example and are illustrative.

## 1. First-Principles Mental Model & Intuition

### Building the alcoves with the customer watching

A joiner fitting alcove shelving shows the customer the first unit before building the rest, so a wrong shelf height is caught early. When the customer says "while you're here, could you also box in the pipes?", the joiner says "yes, that is an extra half-day, here is the price", rather than doing it free and running late on the agreed work. At the end, the customer gets the paint code, care instructions, and a walk-round, and they sign off that it is finished. Early demos, priced changes, and a proper handover are the whole craft of delivery.

```text
 week 1                 week 2                  week 3               handover
 kick-off --> build --> DEMO (feedback round 1) --> refine --> DEMO (round 2) --> training --> acceptance test
                 |                                    |
                 +--- change request? -> price + days -> client approves -> added to plan
 status each Friday: done | next | blockers | decisions        RAG: green / amber (due soon) / red (overdue)
 handover pack: user guide, admin guide, runbook, credentials, training, access removed, acceptance signed
 acceptance: median pricing time after delivery <= 10 minutes (baseline 20)
```

### Feedback early, cheap; feedback late, expensive

Changing a design after a 20-minute demo costs minutes; changing it after the client has trained staff on it costs days. AI systems especially need early contact with real data and real users, because their behaviour on real inputs is the main uncertainty, Module 8.12.

## 2. Core Theory, Mathematical Foundations & Architecture Internals

### Milestones and status

A project plan is a list of milestones with due dates, Module 11.8. At each weekly update, Module 11.10, each milestone is **done**, **on track**, **due soon**, or **overdue**, and the project as a whole gets a simple **RAG** status:

- **Red** if anything is overdue.
- **Amber** if something is due within a couple of days and not done.
- **Green** otherwise.

Report the status honestly, with the reason and the plan, especially when it is amber or red. Clients forgive delays they hear about early; they do not forgive surprises.

### Demos and feedback loops

Schedule demos at milestones, using the client's own data, Module 11.5. Each demo is a **feedback round**: collect all comments, agree which to act on, and confirm in writing. The package sets the number of rounds, Module 11.6; tracking rounds used per deliverable shows when a request goes beyond the agreement:

$$\text{rounds left} = \text{allowed} - \text{used}$$

A negative number means extra rounds are being given away. Either price them or agree that the deliverable is accepted as it is.

For AI tools, feedback should be gathered on **samples**: run the tool on, say, twenty real quotes and review the outputs together, rather than reacting to one example. That turns opinions into evidence, the same principle as evaluation sets, Module 8.12.

### Scope creep and change requests

**Scope creep**, the gradual expansion of work beyond what was agreed, usually arrives as small, reasonable-sounding requests. Each one is either in the package's deliverables, a listed exclusion, or something not mentioned, Module 11.6; the last two need a **change request**: a short written description, a price, and the effect on the timeline, approved by the client before work starts:

$$\text{price} = \left\lceil \frac{h \times r \times (1 + b)}{s} \right\rceil \times s, \qquad \text{extra days} = \left\lceil \frac{h}{\text{hours per day}} \right\rceil$$

Keep a change log; only approved requests count towards the final invoice. Saying "yes, and here is what it costs" is not unfriendly; it protects the agreed work and respects the client's budget decisions.

### Documentation and handover

The client must be able to use, look after, and recover the system without you. A handover pack typically includes:

- **User guide:** how staff use it day to day.
- **Admin guide:** how to change settings, prompts, price lists, users.
- **Runbook for failures:** what to do when it breaks, Module 10.14, and whom to call.
- **Credentials transferred:** accounts and API keys owned by the client, not you.
- **Training delivered:** a session with the people who will use it.
- **Access removed:** your access revoked or reduced to what a support agreement needs, Module 11.10.
- **Acceptance signed:** written confirmation that the work is complete.

### Acceptance against the success measure

At kick-off, Sam and Priya agreed a success measure: pricing time per quote of ten minutes or less, against a baseline of twenty. Acceptance tests it on real use, for example timing the next several quotes. The **median** is a robust summary, because one unusually complex quote should not decide the result:

$$\text{met} \iff \operatorname{median}(t_1, \dots, t_n) \le \text{target}$$

and the improvement against the baseline becomes the headline of the case study, Module 11.5. If the measure is not met, the honest response is to find out why, fix it within scope, or agree a revised target, rather than declaring success.

### Weekly updates that build trust

The weekly update agreed at onboarding, Module 11.10, is the backbone of delivery. Keep it short and in the same shape every week: the overall status with one sentence of explanation; what was done since the last update; what happens next, with dates; blockers, especially anything waiting on the client, such as access or sample data; and decisions needed, each with the options and a recommendation. Writing "waiting on the supplier price list since Tuesday" in a dated update is far more effective than mentioning it on a call, because it creates a record and makes the client's part of the schedule visible.

For AI projects, add a line on quality: how the tool performed on the latest sample of real cases, for example "18 of 20 quotes priced correctly; the two errors were discontinued items, fix in progress". Clients learn to trust a system whose weaknesses are reported openly and fixed visibly.

### Handling disagreements

Disagreements during delivery usually concern scope, quality, or time. Return to the written record: the package's deliverables and exclusions for scope, the agreed success measure and the sample results for quality, and the milestone plan and change log for time. Most disagreements dissolve when both sides look at the same document. Where the record is genuinely unclear, propose a fair compromise quickly, record it, and improve the template for the next client, rather than letting a small dispute colour the whole project.

### Complexity

Milestone status and revision counts are linear in items; change requests are constant time each; the handover check is linear in the checklist; the median needs a sort, $O(n \log n)$.

## 3. Production-Grade Implementation & Annotated Walkthrough

First, milestone status.

```python
import math, statistics
from datetime import date

MILESTONES = [{"name": "kick-off", "due": "2026-11-18", "done": True},
              {"name": "pricing assistant demo", "due": "2026-11-25", "done": True},
              {"name": "staff training", "due": "2026-11-30", "done": False},
              {"name": "handover", "due": "2026-12-01", "done": False}]

def milestone_status(ms, today, soon_days=2):
    t = date.fromisoformat(today)
    out = []
    for m in ms:
        d = date.fromisoformat(m["due"])
        if m["done"]:
            s = "done"
        elif d < t:
            s = "overdue"
        elif (d - t).days <= soon_days:
            s = "due soon"
        else:
            s = "on track"
        out.append((m["name"], s))
    overall = "red" if any(s == "overdue" for _, s in out) else ("amber" if any(s == "due soon" for _, s in out) else "green")
    return out, overall

print(milestone_status(MILESTONES, "2026-11-28"))
print(milestone_status(MILESTONES, "2026-12-02")[1])
```

- **On 28 November, training is due soon, so the project is amber.**
- **On 2 December, with training and handover not done, it is red.** The update must say why and what happens next.

Next, revision rounds.

```python
def revisions_left(log, allowed):
    used = {}
    for item in log:
        used[item] = used.get(item, 0) + 1
    return {k: allowed - v for k, v in used.items()}

print(revisions_left(["pricing assistant", "pricing assistant", "pricing assistant", "training notes"], 2))
```

- **The pricing assistant has had three rounds against two allowed:** one round over.
- **Training notes have one round left.**
- **The fix is a conversation:** accept the current version, or price a further round.

Then, pricing a change request.

```python
def change_request(title, hours, rate, buffer=0.2, step=25):
    price = int(math.ceil(hours * rate * (1 + buffer) / step) * step)
    return {"title": title, "price": price, "extra_days": math.ceil(hours / 6), "status": "awaiting approval"}

def approved_total(crs):
    return sum(c["price"] for c in crs if c["status"] == "approved")

cr = change_request("supplier price sync", 14, 55)
print(cr)
print(approved_total([{**cr, "status": "approved"}, change_request("website form", 6, 55)]))
```

- **Supplier price sync: 14 hours, GBP 925, three extra days.**
- **Only approved requests count:** GBP 925, not including the website form still awaiting a decision.
- **The same item is the Quote System's extra deliverable,** Module 11.6, so upgrading the package is the alternative to offer.

Then, the handover checklist.

```python
HANDOVER = ["user guide", "admin guide", "runbook for failures", "credentials transferred",
            "training delivered", "access removed", "acceptance signed"]

def handover_missing(done):
    return [h for h in HANDOVER if h not in done]

print(handover_missing(["user guide", "training delivered"]))
```

- **Five items are missing,** including the runbook and access removal, the two most often forgotten.
- **Credentials must be the client's,** so the system keeps working if Sam is unavailable.

Finally, the acceptance test.

```python
def acceptance(times_after, target, baseline):
    med = statistics.median(times_after)
    return {"median": med, "met": med <= target, "improvement_pct": round((baseline - med) / baseline * 100)}

print(acceptance([7, 6, 9, 5, 12, 6, 8], 10, 20))
print(acceptance([11, 12, 9], 10, 20))
```

- **Seven timed quotes have a median of 7 minutes: target met, a 65 per cent improvement.** The 12-minute outlier does not change the result.
- **A median of 11 misses the target,** even though it is a 45 per cent improvement; that calls for investigation, not a declaration of success.

**Complexity.** Linear apart from the median's sort.

## 4. Practitioner Wisdom & Edge Cases (Reddit / HN / Stack Overflow Synthesis)

**Building for weeks before the first demo.** Show something early.

**Feedback on one example.** Review a sample of real outputs.

**Unlimited rounds by default.** Track rounds against the package.

**Doing small extras for free.** They add up. Price them or decline.

**Verbal approvals.** Confirm decisions and change requests in writing.

**Hiding delays.** Report amber and red early with a plan.

**Credentials in your name.** Transfer ownership to the client.

**No runbook.** The first failure becomes an emergency call.

**Leaving access open.** Remove it at handover.

**Declaring success without measuring.** Test against the agreed measure.

**Means skewed by outliers.** Use the median.

**Skipping training.** Untrained staff stop using the tool.
