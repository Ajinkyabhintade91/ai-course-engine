# WhatsApp Automation

In many countries WhatsApp is how customers talk to businesses: to ask whether a product is in stock, to check on a delivery, to book an appointment. Automating those conversations can save a business hours a day and answer customers at midnight. It is also more tightly controlled than email. Meta decides which messages a business may send and when, charges for most of them, reviews message templates before they can be used, and can restrict accounts that annoy their users.

This chapter explains the WhatsApp Business Platform as an automation builder meets it: the Cloud API, the 24-hour customer service window, message templates and their categories, how pricing works, how incoming messages reach your workflow through webhooks, and the opt-in and policy rules that keep an account healthy. The lab builds the decisions an automation has to make on every message.

## 1. First-Principles Mental Model & Intuition

### A shop with a doorbell and a noticeboard

Imagine a shop with two ways of talking to customers. When a customer rings the doorbell, the shopkeeper can chat freely about anything for as long as the customer keeps coming back; the conversation stays open for 24 hours after the customer's last word. To contact a customer who has not rung, the shopkeeper may only send a card from a pre-printed set, approved in advance by the shopping centre's management, and only to customers who signed up to receive cards.

That is WhatsApp's model.

```text
   CUSTOMER WRITES FIRST                              BUSINESS WRITES FIRST
   "Do you have the oak table in stock?"              (no recent message from the customer)
          |                                                    |
          v                                                    v
   24-hour customer service window opens         only an approved TEMPLATE may be sent
   (reset by each customer message)                     marketing / utility / authentication
          |                                                    |
          v                                                    v
   free-form replies allowed: text, images,      customer must have opted in
   buttons, lists                                 charged per delivered message
```

### The pieces

| Piece | What it is |
|---|---|
| **WhatsApp Business Platform** | Meta's service for businesses to message customers at scale |
| **Cloud API** | The HTTP API, hosted by Meta, that sends and receives messages |
| **Business phone number** | The number customers see; one account can have several |
| **Template** | A pre-approved message with numbered placeholders, in one of three categories |
| **Customer service window** | 24 hours after the customer's last message, when free-form replies are allowed |
| **Webhook** | How Meta delivers incoming messages and delivery statuses to your workflow |
| **Opt-in** | The customer's agreement to receive messages from the business |

## 2. Core Theory, Mathematical Foundations & Architecture Internals

### The customer service window

Meta's pricing documentation defines it precisely: when a WhatsApp user messages or calls a business, a 24-hour timer called the **customer service window** starts, and each further message or call from the user resets it to 24 hours. While it is open, the business may send **non-template** messages, ordinary text, images, documents, and interactive buttons and lists. When it is closed, the business may send only **template** messages.

For an automation, this is a rule to check before every send. A customer asks a question at 09:00 on Monday; the window is open until 09:00 on Tuesday. A reply drafted at 08:30 on Tuesday may go out as free text with 30 minutes to spare. A reply at 09:01 must use an approved template, typically one that says "We have an update on your question, reply to this message to continue", which, once the customer replies, reopens the window.

### Templates and their categories

A **template** is a message registered in advance and reviewed by Meta, with numbered placeholders for the parts that change:

```text
   name:      order_shipped
   language:  en_GB
   category:  UTILITY
   body:      Hi {{1}}, your order {{2}} has shipped and should arrive on {{3}}.
```

To send it, the automation supplies the template's name and language and the values for each placeholder, in order. Meta classifies templates into three categories, which determine both the rules and the price:

| Category | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing | Promotional | "Our winter sale starts tomorrow" |
| Utility | Transactional or informational, about something the customer did | "Your order has shipped" |
| Authentication | Identity verification | "Your code is 482913" |

A template whose content does not match its category, a promotion dressed as an order update for example, can be recategorised or rejected. Placeholder values have their own rules: Meta rejects body parameters containing new lines or tabs, or more than four consecutive spaces, and every placeholder needs a value. Checking these before sending turns an API error into a clear local message.

### Pricing

Meta moved WhatsApp to **per-message pricing** on 1 July 2025. Its documentation, at the time of writing (November 2026), describes the rules:

1. Meta charges only for messages from the business to users, never for messages from users.
2. A message is charged only when it is **delivered**.
3. The rate depends on the message's **category** and the recipient's **market**, the country or region of their phone number, according to Meta's rate card.
4. Template messages are charged as marketing, utility, or authentication.
5. Non-template replies inside the customer service window are **service** messages. Each business phone number gets **1,000 free service messages a month**, and from 1 October 2026 service messages beyond that free tier are charged.
6. Messages sent in a **free entry point window**, opened when a customer starts a conversation from a click-to-WhatsApp ad and the business replies within the service window, are not charged for marketing, utility, authentication, or service messages while that window lasts, which can be up to 7 days.

Utility and authentication messages also have volume tiers, with lower rates for higher volumes. Because rates vary by market and change on a published calendar, a cost estimate should take its rates from the current rate card, never from a tutorial.

The cost of a month is then a sum over delivered messages:

$$\text{bill} = \sum_{c \in \{\text{marketing}, \text{utility}, \text{authentication}\}} n_c \, r_c + \max(0, n_{\text{service}} - 1000) \, r_{\text{service}}$$

where $n_c$ is the number of delivered messages of category $c$ and $r_c$ its rate, per market, ignoring free-entry-point messages. The lab computes this with round illustrative rates, invented for the exercise.

### Sending and receiving

Sending is a `POST` to the Cloud API's messages endpoint for the business phone number, with a bearer token and a JSON body. A template send names the template and language and lists the parameter values:

```text
   {"messaging_product": "whatsapp",
    "to": "447700900123",
    "type": "template",
    "template": {"name": "order_shipped", "language": {"code": "en_GB"},
                 "components": [{"type": "body", "parameters": [
                     {"type": "text", "text": "Priya"},
                     {"type": "text", "text": "A1234"},
                     {"type": "text", "text": "Thursday 19 November"}]}]}}
```

Receiving uses **webhooks**, the mechanism from Module 7.4, with Meta's own conventions:

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Subscribe | Meta sends a `GET` with `hub.mode=subscribe`, `hub.verify_token`, and `hub.challenge`; your endpoint checks the token matches the one you configured and replies with the challenge value |
| Notify | Meta sends a `POST` with a JSON body for each batch of events: incoming messages, and statuses such as sent, delivered, read, and failed |
| Sign | Each `POST` carries an `X-Hub-Signature-256` header: `sha256=` followed by the HMAC-SHA256 of the raw body, keyed with your app secret |
| Retry | Failed deliveries are retried, for WhatsApp up to 7 days, so the same event can arrive more than once |

Everything Module 7.4 taught applies: verify the signature on the raw bytes with a constant-time comparison, acknowledge quickly and process in the background, and deduplicate by message ID, because retries mean duplicates.

### Opt-in and policy

A business may message only people who have **opted in**. Meta's opt-in guidance requires that the person is told clearly that they are opting in to receive messages, and from which business, and that the opt-in complies with applicable law; the method, a website checkbox, a form, an SMS, in person, is the business's choice. Opt-outs must be honoured: a customer who replies "STOP" or asks not to be contacted must not receive further marketing.

Meta's Business Messaging Policy adds rules about content and behaviour, and Meta monitors quality signals: if many recipients block a business or report its messages, the phone number's quality rating falls and its messaging limits can be reduced. Sending only wanted messages is therefore not just courtesy but how an account stays usable.

AI-powered conversations have their own rule. As TechCrunch reported in October 2025, Meta changed the WhatsApp Business Solution terms to bar AI providers from distributing general-purpose AI assistants through the platform, with the change applying to existing users from 15 January 2026. Businesses using AI to support their own customers, answering questions about orders, products, and bookings, remain within the rules. An automation that answers anything a user asks, unrelated to the business, is the kind the terms exclude.

### Designing the flow

```text
   INBOUND                                        OUTBOUND
   webhook POST                                   event: order shipped
     verify signature, acknowledge                  customer opted in?   no -> stop
     dedupe by message ID                           window open?         yes -> free-form text allowed
     record: last_user_message_at = now                                  no  -> utility template
     classify (AI): stock / delivery / other        render template, check parameters
     answer within window, or hand to a person     send; record message ID; await status webhook
```

### Complexity

Window checks and charge decisions are constant time per message. A monthly bill is linear in the number of messages. Signature verification is linear in the body size. Consent tracking is linear in the number of events.

## 3. Production-Grade Implementation & Annotated Walkthrough

First, the service window.

```python
from datetime import datetime, timedelta

WINDOW = timedelta(hours=24)

def window_state(last_user_message, now):
    if last_user_message is None or now - last_user_message >= WINDOW:
        return "closed", timedelta(0)
    return "open", WINDOW - (now - last_user_message)

start = datetime(2026, 11, 16, 9, 0)
print(window_state(start, start + timedelta(hours=23, minutes=30)))
print(window_state(start, start + timedelta(hours=24)))
print(window_state(None, start))
```

- **The window runs 24 hours from the customer's last message.** The automation stores that time for each customer, updating it on every inbound message.
- **At 23 hours 30 minutes the window is open, with 30 minutes left.** The remaining time is useful for deciding whether a slow task, such as checking stock with a supplier, will finish in time.
- **At exactly 24 hours it is closed.** Treating the boundary as closed is the safe side.
- **A customer who has never written has no window.** Only templates can reach them.

Next, charges for a month.

```python
def message_charge(kind, category, window_open, free_entry, service_used):
    if kind == "freeform" and not window_open:
        raise ValueError("free-form messages need an open customer service window")
    if kind == "freeform":
        category = "service"
    if free_entry:
        return 0, "free entry point"
    if category == "service":
        return (0, "free service tier") if service_used < 1000 else (1, "service")
    return 1, category

def monthly_bill(messages, rates):
    used, total, counts = 0, 0.0, {}
    for message in messages:
        charged, label = message_charge(message["kind"], message.get("category"), message["window_open"],
                                        message.get("free_entry", False), used)
        if message["kind"] == "freeform" or message.get("category") == "service":
            used += 1
        if charged:
            total += rates[label]
            counts[label] = counts.get(label, 0) + 1
    return round(total, 2), counts

RATES = {"marketing": 0.05, "utility": 0.01, "authentication": 0.01, "service": 0.004}
MONTH = ([{"kind": "freeform", "window_open": True}] * 1500
         + [{"kind": "template", "category": "utility", "window_open": False}] * 800
         + [{"kind": "template", "category": "marketing", "window_open": False}] * 300
         + [{"kind": "template", "category": "marketing", "window_open": True, "free_entry": True}] * 50)
print(monthly_bill(MONTH, RATES))
```

- **A free-form message outside the window is an error, not a charge.** The API would refuse it, so the code refuses it first.
- **Free-form replies are service messages.** The first 1,000 in the month are free; from the 1,001st they are charged.
- **Messages in a free entry point window cost nothing.** The 50 marketing replies to ad-driven conversations are free.
- **Templates are charged by category.** Utility and marketing here.
- **The month costs USD 25.00 at these made-up rates.** 500 charged service messages at 0.004, 800 utility at 0.01, and 300 marketing at 0.05 come to 2 + 8 + 15. Marketing is 300 messages out of 2,650 but 60 percent of the bill.
- **Real rates come from Meta's rate card, per market.** These round numbers exist only to make the arithmetic visible.

Then, a template payload.

```python
import json
import re

PLACEHOLDER = re.compile(r"\{\{(\d+)\}\}")

def template_payload(to, name, language, body, params):
    numbers = sorted({int(n) for n in PLACEHOLDER.findall(body)})
    if numbers != list(range(1, len(numbers) + 1)):
        raise ValueError("placeholders must be numbered 1, 2, 3 in sequence")
    if len(params) != len(numbers):
        raise ValueError(f"template needs {len(numbers)} parameters, got {len(params)}")
    for value in params:
        if not value.strip() or "\n" in value or "\t" in value or "     " in value:
            raise ValueError("bad parameter: " + repr(value))
    preview = PLACEHOLDER.sub(lambda match: params[int(match.group(1)) - 1], body)
    payload = {"messaging_product": "whatsapp", "to": to, "type": "template",
               "template": {"name": name, "language": {"code": language},
                            "components": [{"type": "body", "parameters": [{"type": "text", "text": value} for value in params]}]}}
    return payload, preview

BODY = "Hi {{1}}, your order {{2}} has shipped and should arrive on {{3}}."
payload, preview = template_payload("447700900123", "order_shipped", "en_GB", BODY, ["Priya", "A1234", "Thursday 19 November"])
print(preview)
print(json.dumps(payload)[:110])
```

- **Placeholders must be numbered 1, 2, 3 without gaps.** A body with `{{1}}` and `{{3}}` is a broken template.
- **The number of values must match.** Too few or too many is caught here, not by a failed send.
- **Values are checked for the characters Meta rejects.** Empty values, new lines, tabs, and five or more consecutive spaces.
- **The preview shows exactly what the customer will read.** Logging it makes support conversations easier: "what did we send?" has an answer.
- **The payload follows the Cloud API's template shape.** Name, language code, and the body parameters in order.

Finally, webhooks and consent.

```python
import hashlib
import hmac

def handshake(params, verify_token):
    if params.get("hub.mode") == "subscribe" and hmac.compare_digest(params.get("hub.verify_token", ""), verify_token):
        return params.get("hub.challenge")
    return None

def valid_signature(app_secret, body, header):
    if not header or not header.startswith("sha256="):
        return False
    expected = hmac.new(app_secret.encode("utf-8"), body, hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()
    return hmac.compare_digest(expected, header[len("sha256="):])

SECRET = "app-secret-for-tests"
RAW_BODY = b'{"object":"whatsapp_business_account","entry":[]}'
header = "sha256=" + hmac.new(SECRET.encode("utf-8"), RAW_BODY, hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()
print(handshake({"hub.mode": "subscribe", "hub.verify_token": "tok", "hub.challenge": "1158201444"}, "tok"))
print(valid_signature(SECRET, RAW_BODY, header), valid_signature(SECRET, RAW_BODY + b" ", header))

STOP_WORDS = {"stop", "unsubscribe", "opt out"}
START_WORDS = {"start", "subscribe", "opt in"}

def consent(events):
    state = {}
    for number, kind, text in events:
        word = text.strip().lower()
        if kind == "form_opt_in":
            state[number] = True
        elif kind == "message" and word in STOP_WORDS:
            state[number] = False
        elif kind == "message" and word in START_WORDS:
            state[number] = True
    return state

EVENTS = [("447700900123", "form_opt_in", ""), ("447700900456", "message", "Hello"),
          ("447700900123", "message", " STOP "), ("447700900789", "form_opt_in", ""), ("447700900123", "message", "start")]
state = consent(EVENTS)
print(state, [state.get(number, False) for number in ("447700900123", "447700900456", "447700900789")])
```

- **The handshake returns the challenge only when the mode and token match.** The token comparison is constant-time, and anything else gets None, which the endpoint turns into a 403.
- **The signature is an HMAC-SHA256 of the raw body with the app secret.** One extra byte, a space, makes it invalid, which is why the raw bytes must be verified before any parsing, as Module 7.4 explained.
- **`consent` replays opt-in and opt-out events in order.** A form opt-in grants consent, "STOP" removes it, and "start" restores it, with spaces and capitals ignored.
- **Messaging the business is not consent to marketing.** The customer who wrote "Hello" has an open service window but no marketing opt-in, so `state.get` returns False.
- **The first customer opted in, opted out, and opted back in.** The latest event wins, so marketing may be sent again.

**Complexity.** `window_state` and `message_charge` are constant. `monthly_bill` is linear in the messages. `template_payload` is linear in the body and parameters. `valid_signature` is linear in the body. `consent` is linear in the events.

## 4. Practitioner Wisdom & Edge Cases (Reddit / HN / Stack Overflow Synthesis)

**Free text outside the window.** The API rejects it. Check the window before every free-form send and fall back to a template.

**Slow replies that miss the window.** A human hand-off that takes a day turns a free reply into a template send. Track time remaining and prioritise conversations close to expiry.

**Marketing disguised as utility.** Templates whose content does not fit their category get recategorised or rejected. Keep promotions in marketing templates.

**Messaging people who never opted in.** Writing to a number from a list is not consent. Record opt-in with its source and date.

**Ignoring STOP.** Continuing to message someone who opted out generates blocks and reports and damages the number's quality rating.

**Skipping signature verification.** Anyone who finds the webhook URL can send fake messages. Verify `X-Hub-Signature-256` on the raw body.

**Duplicate processing on retries.** Meta retries failed webhook deliveries, so the same message arrives again. Deduplicate by message ID.

**Slow webhook handlers.** Doing the AI call before acknowledging makes deliveries time out and retry. Acknowledge first, process in the background.

**Temporary tokens in production.** Test tokens expire within a day. Use a long-lived system user token stored as a credential.

**Template parameters with line breaks.** Addresses and notes often contain new lines. Clean values before sending.

**Assuming a single global price.** Rates vary by recipient market and category. Estimate per market from the current rate card.

**A general-purpose chatbot on a business number.** Meta's terms exclude general-purpose AI assistants offered by AI providers. Keep AI replies focused on the business's own customers and services.

**Question 3.** A developer's WhatsApp webhook endpoint returns 200 to every request, parses the JSON immediately, calls a language model to draft a reply, and then responds. Customers sometimes receive two identical replies, and a security review finds the endpoint would accept a forged message. Explain both problems and give the corrected design, including the subscription handshake.

<details>
<summary>View Answer</summary>

Duplicate replies: the handler calls the model before responding, which can take several seconds. If Meta's delivery times out, or any request fails, Meta retries the same notification, and the handler processes it again and drafts and sends a second reply. Even without timeouts, retries mean the same message can arrive more than once, and the handler has no deduplication. Forgery: the endpoint never checks the `X-Hub-Signature-256` header, so anyone who discovers the URL can post a fake "incoming message" and make the automation reply, possibly to any number, at the business's expense and under its name. Corrected design: for the subscription `GET`, return the `hub.challenge` value only if `hub.mode` is `subscribe` and `hub.verify_token` matches the configured token, compared in constant time, and otherwise return 403. import hashlib
import hmac
import json
import re
from datetime import datetime, timedelta

WINDOW = timedelta(hours=24)
RATES = {"marketing": 0.05, "utility": 0.01, "authentication": 0.01, "service": 0.004}

START_WORDS = {"start", "subscribe", "opt in"}


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 1
def window_state(last_user_message, now):
    """24 hours from the customer's last message; the boundary counts as closed."""
    if last_user_message is None or now - last_user_message >= WINDOW:
        return "closed", timedelta(0)
    return "open", WINDOW - (now - last_user_message)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 2
def message_charge(kind, category, window_open, free_entry, service_used):
    """Per delivered message: free entry point windows are free, the first 1,000 service
    messages a month are free, templates are charged by category."""
    if kind == "freeform" and not window_open:
        raise ValueError("free-form messages need an open customer service window")
    if kind == "freeform":
        category = "service"
    if free_entry:
        return 0, "free entry point"
    if category == "service":
        return (0, "free service tier") if service_used < 1000 else (1, "service")
    return 1, category


def monthly_bill(messages, rates):
    """Rates are per market on Meta's rate card; these are illustrative."""
    used, total, counts = 0, 0.0, {}
    for message in messages:
        charged, label = message_charge(message["kind"], message.get("category"), message["window_open"],
                                        message.get("free_entry", False), used)
        if message["kind"] == "freeform" or message.get("category") == "service":
            used += 1
        if charged:
            total += rates[label]
            counts[label] = counts.get(label, 0) + 1
    return round(total, 2), counts


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 3
def template_payload(to, name, language, body, params):
    """Catch broken templates and rejected parameter values before the API does."""
    numbers = sorted({int(n) for n in PLACEHOLDER.findall(body)})
    if numbers != list(range(1, len(numbers) + 1)):
        raise ValueError("placeholders must be numbered 1, 2, 3 in sequence")
    if len(params) != len(numbers):
        raise ValueError(f"template needs {len(numbers)} parameters, got {len(params)}")
    for value in params:
        if not value.strip() or "\n" in value or "\t" in value or "     " in value:
            raise ValueError("bad parameter: " + repr(value))
    preview = PLACEHOLDER.sub(lambda match: params[int(match.group(1)) - 1], body)
    payload = {"messaging_product": "whatsapp", "to": to, "type": "template",
               "template": {"name": name, "language": {"code": language},
                            "components": [{"type": "body", "parameters": [{"type": "text", "text": value} for value in params]}]}}
    return payload, preview


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 4
def handshake(params, verify_token):
    """Echo the challenge only for a subscribe request carrying our token."""
    if params.get("hub.mode") == "subscribe" and hmac.compare_digest(params.get("hub.verify_token", ""), verify_token):
        return params.get("hub.challenge")
    return None


def valid_signature(app_secret, body, header):
    """X-Hub-Signature-256: sha256=HMAC of the raw body with the app secret, compared in constant time."""
    if not header or not header.startswith("sha256="):
        return False
    expected = hmac.new(app_secret.encode("utf-8"), body, hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()
    return hmac.compare_digest(expected, header[len("sha256="):])


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 5
def consent(events):
    """The latest opt-in or opt-out wins; writing to the business is not marketing consent."""
    state = {}
    for number, kind, text in events:
        word = text.strip().lower()
        if kind == "form_opt_in":
            state[number] = True
        elif kind == "message" and word in STOP_WORDS:
            state[number] = False
        elif kind == "message" and word in START_WORDS:
            state[number] = True
    return state
