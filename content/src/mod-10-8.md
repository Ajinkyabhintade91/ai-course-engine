# Mistral

**Mistral AI**, the Paris-based company founded in 2023, is Europe's best-known model developer and one of the most prolific publishers of open weights. Its first model, Mistral 7B, set a standard for small models; Mixtral popularised mixture of experts in open models; and its current range spans tiny edge models, a family of 24B models that fit a single consumer GPU, coding-agent models, reasoning models, and large open-weight flagships. Mistral is also the clearest example of why **licences** must be read model by model: within one company's catalogue there are Apache 2.0 models, models under a modified MIT licence with a commercial condition, and models restricted to non-production use.

Facts are from Mistral's announcements, documentation, model cards, and Ollama's library at the time of writing (November 2026). Mistral releases and retires models often; check its models overview page before relying on any line here.

## 1. First-Principles Mental Model & Intuition

### A European tool maker with three kinds of warranty card

Picture a tool maker that sells its products with one of three **warranty cards**. Most tools come with the green card: use them for anything, including resale, as long as you keep the card (Apache 2.0). Some flagship tools come with an amber card: use them freely, except that a business renting the tool out as a service must sign a separate agreement (the modified MIT licences). A few come with a red card: for testing and research only, not for production work (the Non-Production Licence). The tools themselves are excellent and often compact. The rest of the job is reading the card that came in the box.

```text
 2023 Sep  Mistral 7B                      Apache 2.0      small, efficient, the start
 2023 Dec  Mixtral 8x7B (MoE)              Apache 2.0      open mixture of experts
 2024 May  Codestral 22B                   MNPL (non-production)
 2024 Jul  Mistral NeMo 12B                Apache 2.0      128K context
 2025      Mistral Small 3 / 3.1 / 3.2     Apache 2.0      24B, vision, tools; one GPU
 2025      Magistral Small (reasoning)     Apache 2.0      24B, [THINK] reasoning
 2025 Dec  Mistral 3: Ministral 3 3B/8B/14B, Large 3 (675B MoE)   Apache 2.0
 2025 Dec  Devstral Small 2 (24B)          Apache 2.0      coding agents
           Devstral 2 (123B)               modified MIT
 2026 Mar  Mistral Small 4 (119B MoE, 6B active)   Apache 2.0   data-centre hardware
 2026      Mistral Medium 3.5 (128B)       modified MIT
 2026 Oct  Mistral Large 4 (about 1T)      modified MIT: Model-as-a-Service needs a licence
```

### What "small" means at Mistral

Mistral's names do not map to consumer hardware the way one might expect. **Ministral 3** and the **24B** models run on a desktop GPU. **Mistral Small 4**, despite its name, is a 119B-parameter mixture of experts whose announcement lists a minimum of four data-centre H100 GPUs. Read sizes, not names.

### Reasoning output

Magistral writes its reasoning between `[THINK]` and `[/THINK]` markers, followed by the answer. Ollama separates thinking for supported models, but if you call a model through a raw runtime or receive the text unprocessed, you must split it yourself:

$$\text{output} = \texttt{[THINK]}\ \text{reasoning}\ \texttt{[/THINK]}\ \text{answer}$$

If the closing marker never arrives, because the token limit cut the response short, there is no answer: the response should be treated as incomplete, not shown as an answer. Magistral's card recommends a generous maximum of tokens for this reason.

### Sampling: low temperature for the instruct models

Mistral's cards recommend **low temperatures** for its instruct models. The Small 3.2 card recommends "a relatively low temperature, such as temperature=0.15", and Devstral Small 2's examples use 0.15. Magistral's card recommends temperature 0.7 and top-p 0.95 for its reasoning. These differ sharply from Ollama's generic default of 0.8, Module 10.2, so set them in a Modelfile. The Small 3.2 card also advises adding a system prompt.

### Licences by model

| Licence | Models (examples) | In short |
|---|---|---|
| Apache 2.0 | Mistral 7B, Mixtral, NeMo, Small 3.x, Small 4, Ministral 3, Magistral Small, Devstral Small 2, Large 3 | Any use, keep the licence and notices |
| Modified MIT ("Mistral Ecosystem Open-Weight" for Large 4) | Devstral 2, Medium 3.5, Large 4 | Free to use, modify, and sell, with conditions: for Large 4, businesses operating a Model-as-a-Service offering need a separate commercial licence; Ollama's page notes exceptions for companies with large revenue for Medium 3.5 |
| Mistral Non-Production Licence | Codestral 22B | Non-production use; production or commercial use needs a separate licence |

The categories of use that matter in practice are **research** and testing, **internal** production use, a **commercial** product, and **Model-as-a-Service**, selling access to the model itself. Apache 2.0 allows all four; the modified MIT licences allow most, with conditions to check; the Non-Production Licence allows only the first.

### Hosted models retire; local weights do not

Mistral's models overview lists deprecation and retirement dates for its hosted API models. At the time of writing, it lists Mistral Small 3.2 and NeMo as retired on 31 July 2026, with Small 4 and Ministral 3 8B as replacements, and Large 3 as retiring on 30 April 2027, replaced by Large 4. A service calling a retired API model stops working. Weights you downloaded keep working indefinitely: `mistral-small3.2` on Ollama still runs on your machine. That durability is one of the arguments for local deployment, Module 10.1, though local models also stop receiving improvements.

### Choosing

The selection logic of Modules 10.5 to 10.7 applies, with one more filter: the licence must allow the intended use. Among models that have the needed capabilities, fit memory with headroom, and are licensed for the use, prefer the largest, then test.

### Complexity

Licence checks and output splitting are linear in their input; choosing is linear in the catalogue; the retirement report is linear in deployments.

## 3. Production-Grade Implementation & Annotated Walkthrough

First, what each licence allows.

```python
def licence_allows(licence, use):
    if licence == "apache-2.0":
        return "yes"
    if licence == "mnpl":
        return "yes" if use == "research" else "needs commercial licence"
    if licence == "modified-mit":
        return "needs commercial licence" if use == "maas" else "yes, check licence conditions"
    return "unknown"

for lic in ["apache-2.0", "mnpl", "modified-mit"]:
    print(lic, [licence_allows(lic, use) for use in ["research", "internal", "commercial", "maas"]])
```

- **Apache 2.0 allows every use.**
- **The Non-Production Licence allows research only.** Using Codestral 22B inside a product needs a separate agreement.
- **The modified MIT licences allow most uses, but say "check".** Large 4's condition targets Model-as-a-Service; Medium 3.5's terms have revenue-based exceptions. Read the specific text.

Next, splitting reasoning from the answer.

```python
import re

def split_thinking(text):
    m = re.search(r"\[THINK\](.*?)(\[/THINK\]|$)", text, re.S)
    if not m:
        return "", text.strip()
    thinking = m.group(1).strip()
    answer = text[m.end():].strip() if m.group(2) else ""
    return thinking, answer

print(split_thinking("[THINK]Blades fit SAW-3 if 254 mm.[/THINK]Yes, the 254 mm blade fits."))
print(split_thinking("No reasoning here."), split_thinking("[THINK]still thinking"))
```

- **A complete response splits into reasoning and answer.**
- **No markers means the whole text is the answer.**
- **An unclosed marker means no answer.** The response was cut off; retry with a larger token limit.

Then, Modelfiles that follow the cards.

```python
CATALOGUE = [
    {"name": "mistral:7b", "gb": 4.4, "ctx_k": 32, "caps": {"tools"}, "licence": "apache-2.0"},
    {"name": "mistral-nemo:12b", "gb": 7.1, "ctx_k": 128, "caps": {"tools"}, "licence": "apache-2.0"},
    {"name": "ministral-3:3b", "gb": 3.0, "ctx_k": 256, "caps": {"vision", "tools"}, "licence": "apache-2.0"},
    {"name": "ministral-3:8b", "gb": 6.0, "ctx_k": 256, "caps": {"vision", "tools"}, "licence": "apache-2.0"},
    {"name": "ministral-3:14b", "gb": 9.1, "ctx_k": 256, "caps": {"vision", "tools"}, "licence": "apache-2.0"},
    {"name": "codestral:22b", "gb": 13.0, "ctx_k": 32, "caps": set(), "licence": "mnpl"},
    {"name": "magistral:24b", "gb": 14.0, "ctx_k": 39, "caps": {"tools", "thinking"}, "licence": "apache-2.0"},
    {"name": "mistral-small3.2:24b", "gb": 15.0, "ctx_k": 128, "caps": {"vision", "tools"}, "licence": "apache-2.0"},
    {"name": "devstral-small-2:24b", "gb": 15.0, "ctx_k": 384, "caps": {"vision", "tools"}, "licence": "apache-2.0"},
    {"name": "mistral-medium-3.5:128b", "gb": 80.0, "ctx_k": 256, "caps": {"vision", "tools", "thinking"}, "licence": "modified-mit"},
]
PROFILES = {"mistral-small3.2": {"temperature": 0.15}, "devstral-small-2": {"temperature": 0.15},
            "magistral": {"temperature": 0.7, "top_p": 0.95}}

def modelfile_for(model, ctx_k):
    m = next(x for x in CATALOGUE if x["name"] == model)
    family = model.split(":")[0]
    lines = [f"FROM {model}"] + [f"PARAMETER {k} {v}" for k, v in PROFILES.get(family, {}).items()]
    ctx = min(ctx_k, m["ctx_k"])
    lines.append(f"PARAMETER num_ctx {ctx * 1024}")
    notes = [] if ctx == ctx_k else [f"context capped at {m['ctx_k']}K"]
    return lines, notes

print(modelfile_for("mistral-small3.2:24b", 32))
print(modelfile_for("magistral:24b", 64))
```

- **Small 3.2 gets temperature 0.15 and the requested 32K context.**
- **Magistral gets 0.7 and top-p 0.95, and its context is capped at 39K,** with a note explaining why.
- **Models without a profile get only the context.** Their card should be checked and a profile added.

Then, choosing a model the licence allows.

```python
def choose(catalogue, needs, mem_gb, use, headroom=1.2):
    best = None
    for m in catalogue:
        if not set(needs) <= m["caps"]:
            continue
        if not licence_allows(m["licence"], use).startswith("yes"):
            continue
        if m["gb"] * headroom > mem_gb:
            continue
        if best is None or m["gb"] > best["gb"]:
            best = m
    return best["name"] if best else None

print(choose(CATALOGUE, set(), 16, "commercial"), choose(CATALOGUE, set(), 16, "research"))
print(choose(CATALOGUE, {"thinking"}, 128, "maas"), choose(CATALOGUE, {"thinking"}, 128, "commercial"))
```

- **On 16 GB for a commercial product: Ministral 3 14B.** Codestral would fit but its licence does not allow it; the 24B models need 18 GB with headroom.
- **For research on 16 GB, Codestral 22B is allowed** and is the largest that fits.
- **On 128 GB with thinking: Medium 3.5 for a commercial product, but Magistral for a Model-as-a-Service business,** treating Medium 3.5's modified MIT terms as needing a separate licence for that use.

Finally, the hosted retirement report.

```python
from datetime import date

RETIREMENTS = {"mistral-small-2506": ("2026-07-31", "Mistral Small 4"),
               "open-mistral-nemo": ("2026-07-31", "Ministral 3 8B"),
               "mistral-large-2512": ("2027-04-30", "Mistral Large 4")}

def retirement_report(deployments, today, warn_days=180):
    out = []
    t = date.fromisoformat(today)
    for d in deployments:
        if d["mode"] == "local":
            out.append((d["name"], "local weights: not affected"))
            continue
        if d["api_id"] not in RETIREMENTS:
            out.append((d["name"], "no retirement scheduled"))
            continue
        when, repl = RETIREMENTS[d["api_id"]]
        days = (date.fromisoformat(when) - t).days
        if days < 0:
            out.append((d["name"], f"retired {when}: migrate to {repl}"))
        elif days <= warn_days:
            out.append((d["name"], f"retires in {days} days: plan move to {repl}"))
        else:
            out.append((d["name"], f"retires {when}"))
    return out

DEPLOYMENTS = [{"name": "support bot", "mode": "hosted", "api_id": "mistral-small-2506"},
               {"name": "tagger", "mode": "local", "api_id": "mistral-small-2506"},
               {"name": "analytics", "mode": "hosted", "api_id": "mistral-large-2512"},
               {"name": "coder", "mode": "hosted", "api_id": "devstral-2512"}]
for row in retirement_report(DEPLOYMENTS, "2026-11-17"):
    print(row)
```

- **The hosted support bot is on a retired model.** It must move to the replacement.
- **The local tagger uses the same model but is unaffected.** Its weights are on disk.
- **The analytics service has 164 days left.** Within the 180-day warning window, so plan now.
- **The API identifiers here follow Mistral's naming pattern.** Check the models overview for the exact identifiers you use.

## 4. Practitioner Wisdom & Edge Cases (Reddit / HN / Stack Overflow Synthesis)

**Reading "Small" as small.** Small 4 needs data-centre GPUs. Check the size.

**Using Codestral in a product.** Its licence is non-production. Choose Devstral Small 2 or get a licence.

**Assuming one licence per company.** Mistral uses three. Check each model.

**Default temperature.** Mistral recommends 0.15 for its instruct models. Set it.

**Tool-call template problems.** Community reports describe tool calling failing with mismatched chat templates in some runtimes. Use official tags and current runtimes, and test tool calls specifically.

**Magistral beyond 40K tokens.** Quality degrades, per its card. Keep context modest.

**Cut-off reasoning.** An unclosed `[THINK]` means no answer. Raise the token limit.

**Context figures that disagree.** Ollama and the card differ for Devstral Small 2. Use the card's figure as the safe maximum.

**Hosted retirements.** API models are retired on a schedule. Watch the overview page.

**Old Ollama versions.** New models such as Ministral 3 need recent Ollama versions. Update first.

**Mixtral on modest hardware.** 8x7B is 26 GB. Newer 24B and Ministral models are often better and smaller.

**Benchmark claims.** Vendor figures use vendor harnesses. Test your own tasks.

**Question 2.** The shop's coding helper using Magistral sometimes shows users half a sentence of reasoning and nothing else. Use `split_thinking` and `modelfile_for` to explain and fix it.

<details>
<summary>View Answer</summary>

Magistral writes its reasoning between [THINK] and [/THINK] and then the answer. When the token limit stops generation before the closing marker, the response contains only reasoning. `split_thinking("[THINK]still thinking")` returns the reasoning and an empty answer, which the application should treat as an incomplete response rather than displaying the raw text. The fix has three parts: detect the empty answer and retry or show a polite failure; raise the maximum number of output tokens, since Magistral's card recommends a generous limit because reasoning can be long; and run with the card's settings, which `modelfile_for("magistral:24b", 64)` produces as temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95, and a context capped at 39K, since the card notes quality degrades beyond about 40K tokens. With Ollama's thinking support, the reasoning arrives in a separate field, and the same rule applies: no final content means no answer.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 1
def licence_allows(licence, use):
    """Three licence types, four kinds of use."""
    if licence == "apache-2.0":
        return "yes"
    if licence == "mnpl":
        return "yes" if use == "research" else "needs commercial licence"
    if licence == "modified-mit":
        return "needs commercial licence" if use == "maas" else "yes, check licence conditions"
    return "unknown"


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 2
def split_thinking(text):
    """Separate [THINK] reasoning from the answer; an unclosed block means no answer."""
    m = re.search(r"\[THINK\](.*?)(\[/THINK\]|$)", text, re.S)
    if not m:
        return "", text.strip()
    thinking = m.group(1).strip()
    answer = text[m.end():].strip() if m.group(2) else ""
    return thinking, answer


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 3
def modelfile_for(model, ctx_k):
    """A Modelfile with the card's sampling and a context no larger than the model supports."""
    m = next(x for x in CATALOGUE if x["name"] == model)
    family = model.split(":")[0]
    lines = [f"FROM {model}"] + [f"PARAMETER {k} {v}" for k, v in PROFILES.get(family, {}).items()]
    ctx = min(ctx_k, m["ctx_k"])
    lines.append(f"PARAMETER num_ctx {ctx * 1024}")
    notes = [] if ctx == ctx_k else [f"context capped at {m['ctx_k']}K"]
    return lines, notes


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 4
def choose(catalogue, needs, mem_gb, use, headroom=1.2):
    """Largest model with the capabilities, a licence allowing the use, and room in memory."""
    best = None
    for m in catalogue:
        if not set(needs) <= m["caps"]:
            continue
        if not licence_allows(m["licence"], use).startswith("yes"):
            continue
        if m["gb"] * headroom > mem_gb:
            continue
        if best is None or m["gb"] > best["gb"]:
            best = m
    return best["name"] if best else None


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 5
def retirement_report(deployments, today, warn_days=180):
    """Hosted models retire on published dates; local weights do not."""
    out = []
    t = date.fromisoformat(today)
    for d in deployments:
        if d["mode"] == "local":
            out.append((d["name"], "local weights: not affected"))
            continue
        if d["api_id"] not in RETIREMENTS:
            out.append((d["name"], "no retirement scheduled"))
            continue
        when, repl = RETIREMENTS[d["api_id"]]
        days = (date.fromisoformat(when) - t).days
        if days < 0:
            out.append((d["name"], f"retired {when}: migrate to {repl}"))
        elif days <= warn_days:
            out.append((d["name"], f"retires in {days} days: plan move to {repl}"))
        else:
            out.append((d["name"], f"retires {when}"))
    return out
