# Agent Project 2: AI Sales Agent

A sales development agent, often called an AI SDR after the sales development representatives whose work it imitates, researches potential customers, decides which are worth contacting, drafts personalised messages, and keeps the customer relationship management system, the CRM, up to date. It is one of the most commercially popular agent types, and one of the easiest to get badly wrong: an agent that emails thousands of poorly qualified prospects with invented personal details, ignores opt-outs, and overwrites the sales team's notes can damage a brand, a sending domain, and legal standing in a week.

Module 7.17 built a lead-handling workflow with scoring, deduplication, and routing. This project builds the agent on top: qualification that knows what it does not know, personalisation that cannot invent facts, CRM writes that respect people's edits, and outreach that obeys the law and the inbox providers.

## 1. First-Principles Mental Model & Intuition

### A diligent new sales hire

A good new sales hire does not email everyone in a directory. They study the **ideal customer profile**, ICP: the kind of firm that buys. For each lead they check the facts and note what is missing. They write to a prospect about something real, "I saw you are hiring three site carpenters", never something guessed. They log their work in the CRM without overwriting the account manager's notes. They never email someone who asked not to be contacted, do not send a fourth chaser in a month, and do not send at 11 at night. Big deals go past their manager first.

```text
   lead list (Module 7.17: normalised, deduplicated)
        |
   [research]  web + enrichment -> facts with sources (Module 8.15 discipline)
        |
   [qualify vs ICP]  disqualified | research more | nurture | qualified
        |
   [draft]  template slots filled ONLY from sourced facts; missing fact -> no draft
        |
   [CRM update]  allowed fields only; human-entered values win; change log
        |
   [compliance guard]  suppression list, touches per 30 days, business hours, opt-out text, postal address
        |
   [send plan]  approval for big or new-segment deals; daily cap; pause if spam rate too high
        |
   send -> replies -> human sales team
```

### What makes sales agents risky

| Risk | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Invented personalisation | "Congratulations on your expansion" to a firm that is shrinking: instant credibility loss |
| Volume without qualification | Low reply rates, high spam complaints, a damaged sending domain |
| Ignoring opt-outs | Legal exposure and complaints |
| Overwriting CRM data | Sales team loses trust in the CRM and the agent |
| Autonomy on large deals | A clumsy first message to the most valuable prospect |

## 2. Core Theory, Mathematical Foundations & Architecture Internals

### Lead research and qualification

Qualification compares a lead with the ICP: industry, size, region, and other criteria the sales team defines. The agent's research supplies the values, and four outcomes must be distinguished:

| Outcome | Condition | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Disqualified | A known value is outside the ICP | Record the reason; no outreach |
| Research | Some criteria are unknown | Research tasks for the missing fields |
| Nurture | Fits the ICP, no buying signal | Low-frequency content, not direct outreach |
| Qualified | Fits the ICP and shows an **intent signal** | Personalised outreach |

The key design choice is that **unknown is not the same as yes**. A model asked "is this a good lead?" tends to say yes; a rule that sends unknowns to research instead of outreach keeps volume tied to evidence. Intent signals, such as job postings, a new site opening, or a tender, are what make a message timely, and each must carry a source, as in Module 8.15.

### Personalised drafting without invention

Personalisation is valuable only when true. The safe pattern is a **template with slots**, `{first_name}`, `{company}`, `{intent}`, filled exclusively from a dictionary of **verified facts**, each with a source. If a slot has no fact, or a fact has no source, the draft is not produced; the lead goes back to research. The model's creative role is in writing good templates and choosing which verified fact to lead with, not in generating facts. This turns the hallucination problem from "check every sentence" into "check every slot", which code can do.

### CRM updates

The CRM is shared between the agent and people. Three rules keep it trustworthy:

1. **Field allowlist.** The agent may write only certain fields, such as stage, notes, and research summary; never owner, deal value, or contract terms.
2. **Source priority.** Each value records its source, human, enrichment provider, or agent. A lower-priority source never overwrites a higher one: if a salesperson typed a phone number, the agent does not replace it with a scraped one.
3. **Change log.** Every write records what, who, and when, so a bad batch can be found and reverted.

CRM platforms expose these operations through APIs, for example HubSpot's contacts API for creating, reading, and updating records, and the rules above are implemented in the agent's tool layer, not trusted to the prompt, Module 8.4.

### Compliance

Email marketing law varies by country, and the agent must follow the rules for each recipient's jurisdiction; this chapter uses the US rules as a concrete example because they are clearly documented. The US Federal Trade Commission's CAN-SPAM compliance guide states that the law makes **no exception for business-to-business email**, and lists requirements including accurate header information, subject lines that are not deceptive, identifying the message as an advertisement, including **a valid physical postal address**, giving **a clear way to opt out**, and **honouring opt-outs within 10 business days**. It also states that a business cannot contract away its legal responsibility for email sent on its behalf: using an agent or a vendor does not shift the liability. Other jurisdictions add their own consent rules, which may treat companies and individuals differently; take legal advice for each market, and encode the result as rules the guard enforces.

Beyond law, good practice limits **frequency**, a maximum number of touches per recipient in a rolling window, and **timing**, business hours in the recipient's time zone.

### Deliverability

Inbox providers decide whether outreach is seen at all. Google's email sender guidelines ask senders to keep the spam rate reported in Postmaster Tools **below 0.10 percent and never reach 0.30 percent**, and for senders of 5,000 or more messages a day add requirements including SPF, DKIM, and DMARC authentication and one-click unsubscribe for marketing messages. A spam rate is complaints divided by delivered messages:

$$\text{spam rate} = \frac{\text{complaints}}{\text{delivered}}$$

Three complaints in 1,000 messages is 0.3 percent: the line Google says never to reach. That is a very small number of annoyed recipients, which is why qualification, personalisation, and frequency limits are deliverability measures as much as courtesy. A **daily cap** per mailbox and a **brake** that pauses sending when the spam rate approaches the limit protect the domain.

### Approval and autonomy

Following Module 8.11, outreach is tiered: routine messages to qualified leads in an established segment go out automatically within caps; messages about large potential deals, or to a new segment whose messaging has not been reviewed, go to a person first. The send plan applies these rules each day.

### Complexity

Qualification is linear in criteria. Personalisation is linear in the template's slots. CRM updates are linear in changed fields. The compliance guard is linear in the touch history. The send plan is linear in drafts.

## 3. Production-Grade Implementation & Annotated Walkthrough

First, qualification.

```python
import re

ICP = {"industry": {"construction", "joinery", "facilities"}, "min_employees": 20, "regions": {"UK", "IE"}}

def qualify(lead, icp):
    reasons, unknown = [], []
    checks = [("industry", lambda v: v in icp["industry"]),
              ("employees", lambda v: v >= icp["min_employees"]),
              ("region", lambda v: v in icp["regions"])]
    for field, test in checks:
        value = lead.get(field)
        if value is None:
            unknown.append(field)
        elif not test(value):
            reasons.append(f"{field} {value} outside ICP")
    if reasons:
        return "disqualified", reasons
    if unknown:
        return "research", [f"find {field}" for field in unknown]
    if lead.get("intent"):
        return "qualified", ["fits ICP with an intent signal"]
    return "nurture", ["fits ICP, no intent signal yet"]

LEAD = {"company": "Harper Joinery", "industry": "joinery", "employees": 45, "region": "UK", "intent": "hiring 3 site carpenters"}
print(qualify(LEAD, ICP))
print(qualify({**LEAD, "intent": None}, ICP))
print(qualify({**LEAD, "employees": None, "region": None}, ICP))
print(qualify({**LEAD, "industry": "retail", "employees": 5}, ICP))
```

- **Known values outside the ICP disqualify, with every reason listed.**
- **Unknown values become research tasks, never a guess.** The missing employees and region produce two tasks.
- **Fitting the ICP is not enough for outreach.** Without an intent signal, the lead is nurtured.
- **Harper Joinery is qualified because it is hiring site carpenters.** A timely, sourced reason to write.

Next, personalisation from sourced facts only.

```python
SLOT = re.compile(r"\{(\w+)\}")

def personalise(template, facts):
    slots = SLOT.findall(template)
    missing = [s for s in dict.fromkeys(slots) if s not in facts]
    if missing:
        return None, [f"no verified fact for {s}" for s in missing]
    unsourced = [s for s in dict.fromkeys(slots) if not facts[s].get("source")]
    if unsourced:
        return None, [f"fact {s} has no source" for s in unsourced]
    return SLOT.sub(lambda m: facts[m.group(1)]["value"], template), []

TEMPLATE = "Hi {first_name}, I saw {company} is {intent}. Our SAW-3 kits help new site teams get started."
FACTS = {"first_name": {"value": "Priya", "source": "crm"},
         "company": {"value": "Harper Joinery", "source": "crm"},
         "intent": {"value": "hiring 3 site carpenters", "source": "https://harperjoinery.example/careers"}}
print(personalise(TEMPLATE, FACTS))
print(personalise(TEMPLATE, {k: v for k, v in FACTS.items() if k != "intent"}))
print(personalise(TEMPLATE, {**FACTS, "intent": {"value": "expanding fast", "source": ""}}))
```

- **Every slot must have a fact, and every fact a source.** Otherwise there is no draft and the reasons say why.
- **The full draft mentions the careers page's real hiring news.**
- **Without the intent fact, nothing is produced.** The lead returns to research rather than receiving a generic or invented line.
- **"Expanding fast" with no source is refused.** That is exactly the kind of plausible phrase a model invents.

Then, CRM write rules.

```python
PRIORITY = {"human": 3, "enrichment": 2, "agent": 1}

def crm_update(record, changes, source, allowed, now):
    applied, rejected = [], []
    for field, value in changes.items():
        if field not in allowed:
            rejected.append((field, "field not writable by agent"))
            continue
        current = record.get(field)
        if current and PRIORITY[current["source"]] > PRIORITY[source]:
            rejected.append((field, f"kept {current['source']} value"))
            continue
        if current and current["value"] == value:
            continue
        record[field] = {"value": value, "source": source, "at": now}
        applied.append(field)
    return applied, rejected

RECORD = {"stage": {"value": "new", "source": "agent", "at": 1},
          "phone": {"value": "0161 000 0000", "source": "human", "at": 1}}
print(crm_update(RECORD, {"stage": "researched", "phone": "0161 999 9999", "owner": "agent", "notes": "hiring carpenters"},
                 "agent", {"stage", "phone", "notes"}, now=5))
print(crm_update(RECORD, {"stage": "researched"}, "agent", {"stage"}, now=6))
```

- **The owner field is not on the agent's allowlist.** Rejected.
- **The phone number was typed by a person.** The agent's scraped number does not replace it.
- **Stage and notes are written with source and time.** The record is its own change log.
- **Writing the same value again is a no-op.** No spurious timestamps, and repeated runs are idempotent.

Then, the compliance guard.

```python
SUPPRESSED = {"buyer@oldclient.example"}
ADDRESS = "Toolshop Ltd, 1 Mill Lane, Leeds LS1 4AB"

def outreach_guard(lead, message, history, today, hour_local, max_touches=3, window=30):
    issues = []
    if lead["email"].lower() in SUPPRESSED or lead.get("opted_out"):
        issues.append("opted out or suppressed")
    recent = [day for day in history if today - day < window]
    if len(recent) >= max_touches:
        issues.append(f"{len(recent)} touches in {window} days")
    if not 9 <= hour_local < 18:
        issues.append("outside business hours")
    if "unsubscribe" not in message.lower():
        issues.append("no opt-out instructions")
    if ADDRESS not in message:
        issues.append("no postal address")
    return not issues, issues

MESSAGE = "Hi Priya, I saw Harper Joinery is hiring. Reply unsubscribe to opt out. " + ADDRESS
PRIYA = {"email": "priya@harperjoinery.example"}
print(outreach_guard(PRIYA, MESSAGE, [1, 10], today=20, hour_local=10))
print(outreach_guard(PRIYA, MESSAGE, [1, 10, 15], today=20, hour_local=10))
print(outreach_guard({"email": "Buyer@OldClient.example"}, "Hi", [], today=20, hour_local=20))
```

- **The suppression check is case-insensitive.** `Buyer@OldClient.example` is the suppressed address.
- **Touches are counted in a rolling 30-day window.** A third touch within 30 days blocks a fourth.
- **The hour is the recipient's local time.**
- **Every message must carry opt-out instructions and the postal address.** Two of the requirements in the FTC's guide.
- **All issues are reported together.** The third call fails four ways at once.

Finally, the daily send plan.

```python
def send_plan(drafts, daily_cap, approval_value, spam_rate):
    if spam_rate >= 0.003:
        return {d["id"]: "paused" for d in drafts}, "spam rate at or above 0.30%"
    plan, sent = {}, 0
    for draft in drafts:
        if draft["value"] > approval_value or draft["new_segment"]:
            plan[draft["id"]] = "approval"
        elif sent < daily_cap:
            plan[draft["id"]] = "send"
            sent += 1
        else:
            plan[draft["id"]] = "tomorrow"
    return plan, ("spam rate above 0.10% target" if spam_rate >= 0.001 else "ok")

DRAFTS = [{"id": f"d{i}", "value": v, "new_segment": n}
          for i, (v, n) in enumerate([(2000, False), (12000, False), (1500, True), (900, False), (3000, False), (500, False)], 1)]
print(send_plan(DRAFTS, daily_cap=3, approval_value=10000, spam_rate=0.0004))
print(send_plan(DRAFTS, daily_cap=3, approval_value=10000, spam_rate=0.003))
```

- **A potential deal worth 12,000 goes to approval, as does the first message to a new segment.**
- **Routine drafts are sent up to the cap of three; the rest wait for tomorrow.**
- **At a 0.30 percent spam rate, everything pauses.** Google's guidelines say never to reach it.
- **Between 0.10 and 0.30 percent, sending continues with a warning.** The team should tighten qualification before the brake engages.

## 4. Practitioner Wisdom & Edge Cases (Reddit / HN / Stack Overflow Synthesis)

**Volume over relevance.** Thousands of generic emails burn the domain. Qualify hard and send fewer.

**Guessed personalisation.** One invented detail destroys trust. Fill slots only from sourced facts.

**Unknown treated as fit.** Missing data defaults to "qualified" when a model decides. Route unknowns to research.

**Agents overwriting people.** Sales teams abandon a CRM the agent corrupts. Protect human-entered fields.

**No change log.** A bad batch of updates cannot be undone. Record source and time on every write.

**Case-sensitive suppression lists.** `Buyer@` and `buyer@` are the same person. Normalise addresses.

**"B2B is exempt."** In the US, CAN-SPAM makes no exception for business email. Check each market's rules.

**Liability outsourced to the tool.** The FTC guide is explicit that responsibility cannot be contracted away.

**Sending from the main domain.** A spam problem from outreach can affect invoices and receipts. Many teams send outreach from a separate, authenticated domain.

**Ignoring the spam rate.** Three complaints per thousand is the line. Watch Postmaster Tools and brake early.

**Chasing forever.** A fourth follow-up in a month generates complaints, not replies. Cap touches.

**Big deals on autopilot.** The most valuable prospects deserve a person's review. Route them to approval.

**Question 2.** A salesperson complains that the agent keeps changing a client's phone number to an old switchboard number, and that the deal owner field was set to "agent" overnight. Explain how `crm_update` prevents both, and what the change log adds.

<details>
<summary>View Answer</summary>

The phone number was typed by the salesperson, so its stored source is "human", priority 3, while the agent writes with source "agent", priority 1. `crm_update` compares priorities before writing and rejects the change with "kept human value", so the scraped switchboard number never replaces the verified one; an enrichment provider, priority 2, would likewise be unable to overwrite it. The owner field is not in the agent's allowlist, which in the lab contains only stage, phone, and notes, and in the operating policy only stage, notes, and research summary, so the write is rejected with "field not writable by agent" before any value is compared. Enforcing both rules in the tool layer, rather than asking the model not to do it, means a prompt injection or a model mistake cannot bypass them. The change log, the source and time stored with every value the agent writes, shows exactly which fields the agent changed and when, so if a bad batch does get through, for example notes written from a wrong research result, it can be found by source and timestamp and reverted, and the salesperson can see that the agent did not touch the fields it was not allowed to.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 1
def qualify(lead, icp):
    """Unknown is not yes: unknowns become research tasks."""
    reasons, unknown = [], []
    checks = [("industry", lambda v: v in icp["industry"]),
              ("employees", lambda v: v >= icp["min_employees"]),
              ("region", lambda v: v in icp["regions"])]
    for field, test in checks:
        value = lead.get(field)
        if value is None:
            unknown.append(field)
        elif not test(value):
            reasons.append(f"{field} {value} outside ICP")
    if reasons:
        return "disqualified", reasons
    if unknown:
        return "research", [f"find {field}" for field in unknown]
    if lead.get("intent"):
        return "qualified", ["fits ICP with an intent signal"]
    return "nurture", ["fits ICP, no intent signal yet"]


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 2
def personalise(template, facts):
    """Check every slot, not every sentence: no sourced fact, no draft."""
    slots = SLOT.findall(template)
    missing = [s for s in dict.fromkeys(slots) if s not in facts]
    if missing:
        return None, [f"no verified fact for {s}" for s in missing]
    unsourced = [s for s in dict.fromkeys(slots) if not facts[s].get("source")]
    if unsourced:
        return None, [f"fact {s} has no source" for s in unsourced]
    return SLOT.sub(lambda m: facts[m.group(1)]["value"], template), []


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 3
def crm_update(record, changes, source, allowed, now):
    """Allowlisted fields, source priority, and a record of every write."""
    applied, rejected = [], []
    for field, value in changes.items():
        if field not in allowed:
            rejected.append((field, "field not writable by agent"))
            continue
        current = record.get(field)
        if current and PRIORITY[current["source"]] > PRIORITY[source]:
            rejected.append((field, f"kept {current['source']} value"))
            continue
        if current and current["value"] == value:
            continue
        record[field] = {"value": value, "source": source, "at": now}
        applied.append(field)
    return applied, rejected


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 4
def outreach_guard(lead, message, history, today, hour_local, max_touches=3, window=30):
    """Suppression, frequency, timing, and required content, all reported together."""
    issues = []
    if lead["email"].lower() in SUPPRESSED or lead.get("opted_out"):
        issues.append("opted out or suppressed")
    recent = [day for day in history if today - day < window]
    if len(recent) >= max_touches:
        issues.append(f"{len(recent)} touches in {window} days")
    if not 9 <= hour_local < 18:
        issues.append("outside business hours")
    if "unsubscribe" not in message.lower():
        issues.append("no opt-out instructions")
    if ADDRESS not in message:
        issues.append("no postal address")
    return not issues, issues


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 5
def send_plan(drafts, daily_cap, approval_value, spam_rate):
    """Approvals first, a daily cap, and a brake on the spam rate."""
    if spam_rate >= 0.003:
        return {d["id"]: "paused" for d in drafts}, "spam rate at or above 0.30%"
    plan, sent = {}, 0
    for draft in drafts:
        if draft["value"] > approval_value or draft["new_segment"]:
            plan[draft["id"]] = "approval"
        elif sent < daily_cap:
            plan[draft["id"]] = "send"
            sent += 1
        else:
            plan[draft["id"]] = "tomorrow"
    return plan, ("spam rate above 0.10% target" if spam_rate >= 0.001 else "ok")
