# After Day 30

Thirty days of steady practice produce real skill, a portfolio piece, an offer, and a first round of outreach. They do not produce mastery; nothing does in thirty days. What happens next decides whether the month becomes a career or a memory. This final module of the roadmap covers three things: choosing a **specialisation** so effort compounds in one direction, **staying current** in a field that changes weekly without drowning in news, and **compounding** through projects, an audience, and clients over the following months.

The paths, ratings, and growth rates in the examples are illustrative; replace them with your own as you learn what your market rewards.

## 1. First-Principles Mental Model & Intuition

### After the apprenticeship

After an apprenticeship, a joiner chooses a direction: kitchens, staircases, restoration, or furniture. They keep up with new tools and materials through a few trusted suppliers and trade magazines, not by trying every gadget at every show. And their reputation compounds: each job well done produces photographs, referrals, and repeat customers, so the tenth year is far easier than the first. The same three ideas, direction, filtered learning, and compounding, apply after the roadmap.

```text
 SPECIALISE                 STAY CURRENT                         COMPOUND
 skills vs path needs       weekly digest, not a feed             projects -> case studies
 + market demand            relevant + primary + actionable       audience grows: a(t+1) = a(t)(1+g) + new
 -> 1 path for 6 months     - hype                                enquiries = audience x rate
                            70 build / 20 learn / 10 news         clients -> retainers -> referrals (Unit 11)
 QUARTERLY GOALS: a number, a date, a result (not an activity)
```

### Depth beats breadth after the basics

The roadmap gave breadth: a taste of everything. From here, depth in one direction is what clients pay for, Module 11.4, and what makes learning efficient, because each new thing builds on the last. Breadth remains useful as context, not as a plan.

## 2. Core Theory, Mathematical Foundations & Architecture Internals

### Specialisation paths

Several directions follow naturally from this course:

| Path | Builds on | What you sell |
|---|---|---|
| Automation specialist | Unit 7, Unit 11 | Workflows that remove manual work for a niche |
| Agent engineer | Units 8 and 9 | Agents with tool use, retrieval, and evaluation |
| AI content producer | Units 5 and 6 | Content systems and production for brands |
| Local AI integrator | Units 9 and 10 | Private, on-premises AI for sensitive data |
| AI consultant | Units 5 and 11 | Problem finding, tool selection, process redesign |

A simple way to compare them is to measure the **gap** between your current skills and each path's needs, and weigh it against **demand** in your market:

$$\text{gap} = \sum_{\text{areas}} \max(0,\ \text{need} - \text{score}), \qquad \text{fit} = \frac{\text{demand}}{1 + \text{gap}}$$

A path with strong demand but a large gap may still be right if you are willing to close it; the formula is a starting point for the decision, not the decision. Choose one path for about six months, then review.

### Staying current without drowning in news

AI news is abundant, repetitive, and often hype. A sustainable approach:

- **Batch it:** read once a week, not continuously.
- **Prefer primary sources:** release notes, documentation, papers, and practitioners who build things, over aggregators and influencers.
- **Filter by relevance:** keep items about your path's tools and problems.
- **Keep the actionable:** something you can try or apply this week.
- **Discard hype:** benchmark victories and income claims, Module 11.1, rarely change what you do on Monday.

A scoring rule makes the filter explicit: relevant items score 2, primary sources 1, actionable items 1, hype minus 2; keep only items scoring 3 or more, and at most five a week. For a business owner's perspective, official statistics such as the ONS's reports on AI adoption change more slowly but say more about your market than daily headlines.

### Splitting weekly time

After the roadmap, a useful default is roughly **70 per cent building**, on client work or your own projects, **20 per cent learning** something new for your path, and **10 per cent news** in the weekly batch. The proportions are a guard against the common failure of consuming instead of producing.

### Compounding: projects, audience, and clients

Effort compounds when its results accumulate: each project becomes a case study; each case study attracts readers; readers become enquiries; enquiries become clients; clients become retainers and referrals, Module 11.12. An audience that grows by a fraction $g$ a month from sharing work, plus $n$ new followers from outreach and events, evolves as

$$a_{t+1} = a_t (1 + g) + n$$

Starting from 100 people, with 5 per cent monthly growth and 20 new each month, the audience reaches about 498 in a year, against 340 with the additions alone. If about 1 per cent of the audience sends an enquiry in a month, that is around four enquiries a month by the end of the year, before referrals. Kevin Kelly's essay "1,000 True Fans" makes the broader point that a modest, engaged audience can sustain an independent creator.

### Goals for the next quarter

Goals in the style of objectives and key results, OKRs, keep the next stage focused. A useful goal has a **number**, a **date**, and describes a **result** rather than an activity: "win three retainer clients at GBP 432 a month by 31 March" rather than "learn more about agents". Activities are how you get there; results are how you know you did.

### The day 30 review

Day 30 of the roadmap is the bridge to everything in this module. Spend it on four questions, in writing: what did each weekly deliverable achieve, with numbers where possible; which areas improved most, by re-scoring the six areas from Module 12.1; which specialisation path fits best now, using the comparison above; and what three goals, each with a number and a date, will the next quarter pursue. Schedule the first weekly session of the next quarter before closing the review, so the routine continues without a gap.

### Complexity

Path fit is linear in paths times areas; triage sorts the kept items; the time split is constant; the audience model is linear in months; goal checks are linear in goals.

First, choosing a specialisation.

```python
import math, re

PATHS = {
    "automation specialist": {"needs": {"automation": 3, "business": 2}, "demand": 4},
    "agent engineer": {"needs": {"agents": 4, "coding": 3}, "demand": 5},
    "AI content producer": {"needs": {"tools": 3, "prompting": 3}, "demand": 3},
    "local AI integrator": {"needs": {"coding": 3, "agents": 2, "local": 3}, "demand": 3},
    "AI consultant": {"needs": {"business": 4, "prompting": 3}, "demand": 4},
}

def path_fit(scores, paths=PATHS):
    out = []
    for name, p in paths.items():
        gap = sum(max(0, need - scores.get(area, 0)) for area, need in p["needs"].items())
        out.append((name, gap, round(p["demand"] / (1 + gap), 2)))
    return sorted(out, key=lambda x: (-x[2], x[0]))

SAM = {"prompting": 4, "tools": 4, "automation": 3, "agents": 2, "coding": 3, "business": 2, "local": 1}
for row in path_fit(SAM):
    print(row)
```

- **Automation specialist fits best: no gap and good demand, a fit of 4.0.** It matches Sam's quoting thread.
- **Agent engineer has the highest demand but a gap of 2,** a fit of 1.67; a goal for later.
- **Scores come from the roadmap's self-assessment,** updated after 30 days of practice.

```python
def triage(items, interests, limit=5):
    keep = []
    for it in items:
        score = (2 if any(k in it["topic"] for k in interests) else 0) + (1 if it["primary"] else 0) \
                + (1 if it["actionable"] else 0) - (2 if it["hype"] else 0)
        if score >= 3:
            keep.append((score, it["title"]))
    keep.sort(key=lambda x: -x[0])
    return [t for _, t in keep[:limit]]
```

```python
def time_split(weekly_hours, build=0.7, learn=0.2, news=0.1):
    return {"build": round(weekly_hours * build, 1), "learn": round(weekly_hours * learn, 1),
```

Then, audience growth and enquiries.

```python
def audience(start, monthly_growth, new_per_month, months):
    a = start
    for _ in range(months):
        a = a * (1 + monthly_growth) + new_per_month
    return round(a)

def enquiries(audience_size, rate):
    return math.floor(audience_size * rate)

print(audience(100, 0.05, 20, 12), audience(100, 0.0, 20, 12), enquiries(audience(100, 0.05, 20, 12), 0.01))
```

- **At 1 per cent, about four enquiries a month,** plus referrals from clients.

## 4. Practitioner Wisdom & Edge Cases (Reddit / HN / Stack Overflow Synthesis)

**Stopping after day 30.** Momentum fades fast. Set next quarter's goals on day 30.

**Chasing every new model.** Batch the news weekly.

**Reading summaries of summaries.** Prefer primary sources.

**Specialising too narrowly too soon.** Choose a direction for six months, then review.

**Never specialising.** Generalists compete on price.

**Consuming instead of producing.** Keep about 70 per cent of time for building.

**Expecting audience growth in weeks.** Compounding is slow at first. Keep posting.

**Vanity metrics.** Followers matter less than enquiries and clients.

**Activity goals.** "Learn agents" is not a result.

**Goals without dates.** They drift.

**Ignoring clients' needs when choosing a path.** Demand matters as much as interest.

**Comparing yourself with outliers.** Compare with your last quarter.
