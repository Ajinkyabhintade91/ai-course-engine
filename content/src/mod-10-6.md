# Llama

Meta's **Llama** models did more than any others to create the local-model world. When the first Llama's weights spread in early 2023, they gave researchers and hobbyists a capable base model to run, fine-tune, and study, and an ecosystem grew around them: llama.cpp, named after the model, the fine-tunes that followed, and eventually tools like Ollama. This module covers the Llama lineage up to Llama 4, its sizes and capabilities, Meta's 2026 change of direction, and, most practically, the **Llama licence**: a community licence that allows a great deal but is not open source in the usual sense, with conditions that matter if you build products.

Facts here are from Meta's licence and policy pages, model cards, Ollama's library, and reporting cited at the end, at the time of writing (November 2026). This module explains the licence terms; it is not legal advice, and a product decision should involve someone qualified to give it.

## 1. First-Principles Mental Model & Intuition

### A franchise, not a gift

Using Llama is like taking on a **franchise** rather than receiving a gift. You can run the business, adapt the recipes, and sell the result, and the franchisor charges nothing. But the agreement has conditions: display the brand ("Built with Llama"), keep the agreement with anything you pass on, name new recipes in a particular way ("Llama" first), follow the code of conduct (the acceptable use policy), and if your business becomes enormous (over 700 million monthly users), ask permission first. Some territories have extra terms for some products (the EU and multimodal models). By contrast, an **Apache 2.0** model is closer to a gift with a receipt: keep the receipt (the licence and notices) and do almost anything.

```text
 2023 Feb   Llama 1      7B to 65B, research release; weights spread widely
 2023 Jul   Llama 2      7B, 13B, 70B; commercial use under a community licence
 2024 Apr   Llama 3      8B, 70B; 8K context
 2024 Jul   Llama 3.1    8B, 70B, 405B; 128K context; 8 languages
 2024 Sep   Llama 3.2    1B, 3B text; 11B, 90B vision
 2024 Dec   Llama 3.3    70B text; quality close to 3.1 405B per Meta
 2025 Apr   Llama 4      Scout 109B-A17B (10M context), Maverick 400B-A17B (1M); natively multimodal
            (Behemoth announced, not released)
 2026 Apr   Muse Spark   closed model from Meta; reported as replacing Llama in Meta's products
 2026 Aug   Muse Glimmer 30B open-weight, Apache 2.0, not under the Llama licence
```

### Why the licence deserves a module

For a hobbyist the licence rarely matters. For a business that fine-tunes a model, ships it inside a product, or has customers in the EU, the conditions change what you must do, and sometimes which model you may use at all.

### Reception and Meta's change of direction

Llama 4's launch in April 2025 was contentious: reporting at the time described Meta submitting an experimental chat-tuned version to the LMArena leaderboard, while the public release ranked much lower, and independent tests found weak long-context results despite the very large advertised windows. Local users noted there was no small model. Many continued to run Llama 3.3 70B on large machines and Llama 3.1 8B or 3.2 3B on small ones.

In April 2026, Meta launched **Muse Spark**, a closed model, which reporting described as a pivot away from its open-weights Llama strategy. In August 2026, Meta released **Muse Glimmer**, a 30B open-weight model under **Apache 2.0**, which Ollama lists with vision, tools, and thinking. So "Meta's open model" no longer means "Llama-licensed": check the licence of each model, not the company.

### Role in the ecosystem

Llama's release created the pattern later families followed: open weights, many sizes, community fine-tunes, and quantized community builds. llama.cpp began as a project to run Llama on a laptop and became the engine under much of local AI, including the GGUF format, Module 10.1. Many models were fine-tuned or distilled from Llama, for example DeepSeek's R1 distillations onto Llama 8B and 70B bases, Module 10.9, and each inherits Llama's licence conditions.

### The licence: key clauses

The Llama 3.x and 4 community licences share their main structure. In outline, each grants a royalty-free licence to use, copy, modify, and distribute the "Llama Materials", subject to:

1. **Redistribution.** If you distribute the materials, or a product or model containing them, you must provide a copy of the agreement and prominently display "Built with Llama" on a related website, interface, blog post, about page, or documentation.
2. **Naming derived models.** If you use the materials **or their outputs** to create, train, fine-tune, or improve an AI model that you distribute or make available, you must include "Llama" at the beginning of its name. Training a model on Llama's outputs counts.
3. **Acceptable use.** Use must follow the acceptable use policy, which prohibits, among other things, illegal activity, weapons and critical-infrastructure harm, fraud and disinformation, and failing to disclose known dangers of your system.
4. **Additional commercial terms.** If, on the version's release date, your products had more than 700 million monthly active users in the preceding calendar month, you must request a licence from Meta, which Meta may grant at its sole discretion.
5. **End users of integrated products** receiving the materials as part of a product do not themselves take on the redistribution section.

The **700 million** threshold is evaluated once, against the month before the model version's release date, not continuously. It targets a handful of the largest technology companies.

### The EU restriction for multimodal models

The acceptable use policies for **Llama 3.2** and **Llama 4** state that, for multimodal models, the licence's grant of rights is not given to an individual domiciled in, or a company with its principal place of business in, the European Union. The restriction does not apply to end users of a product or service that incorporates such models. In practice: an EU company may not build with Llama 3.2 Vision or Llama 4 under this licence, while EU customers may use a product built by a non-EU company. Text-only Llama models, such as 3.1, 3.3, and 3.2 1B and 3B, are not covered by this restriction. Llama 4 models are all natively multimodal, so the restriction covers them whichever way you use them.

### Llama licence versus Apache 2.0

| Question | Llama community licence | Apache 2.0 |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial use | Yes, with conditions | Yes |
| Attribution | "Built with Llama" displayed, licence copy | Licence copy, notices, changes marked |
| Naming derived models | Must begin with "Llama" | No requirement |
| Use restrictions | Acceptable use policy | None beyond law |
| Size threshold | 700M MAU, licence on request | None |
| Territory | EU restriction for multimodal models | None |
| OSI open source | No | Yes |

The Open Source Initiative's definition of open source does not allow field-of-use restrictions or discrimination against groups, so the Llama licence, with its use policy and thresholds, is usually described as **open weights** rather than open source.

### Complexity

Each compliance check is constant time per product or model; choosing among $n$ models is $O(n)$.

## 3. Production-Grade Implementation & Annotated Walkthrough

First, the 700 million monthly active user clause.

```python
def needs_meta_licence(mau_by_month, release_month):
    y, m = map(int, release_month.split("-"))
    prev = f"{y - 1}-12" if m == 1 else f"{y}-{m - 1:02d}"
    return mau_by_month.get(prev, 0) > 700_000_000

MAU = {"2024-11": 650_000_000, "2024-12": 720_000_000}
print(needs_meta_licence(MAU, "2025-01"), needs_meta_licence(MAU, "2024-12"))
```

- **Only the calendar month before the release date matters.** For a January release, that is December of the previous year.
- **The threshold is "greater than" 700 million.** Exactly 700 million does not trigger it.
- **A company that grows past the threshold after the release date is not caught by that version's clause.** A later version's clause applies afresh at its own release date.

Next, attribution and naming.

```python
def attribution_issues(product):
    issues = []
    if not product.get("distributes"):
        return issues
    if not product.get("includes_licence_copy"):
        issues.append("include a copy of the licence agreement")
    if not product.get("shows_built_with_llama"):
        issues.append('prominently display "Built with Llama"')
    name = product.get("derived_model_name")
    if name and not name.startswith("Llama"):
        issues.append(f'derived model name must begin with "Llama": {name}')
    return issues

print(attribution_issues({"distributes": True, "includes_licence_copy": True,
                          "shows_built_with_llama": False, "derived_model_name": "ShopBot-8B"}))
```

- **No distribution, no redistribution duties.** Running a model internally does not trigger this section, though the use policy still applies.
- **The shop's planned name, `ShopBot-8B`, breaks the naming rule.** `Llama-ShopBot-8B` would satisfy it.
- **The naming rule also covers models trained on Llama's outputs,** such as a small model distilled from Llama answers.

Then, the EU restriction.

```python
CATALOGUE = [
    {"name": "llama3.2:1b", "licence": "llama3.2", "gb": 1.3, "vision": False, "multimodal_model": False},
    {"name": "llama3.2:3b", "licence": "llama3.2", "gb": 2.0, "vision": False, "multimodal_model": False},
    {"name": "llama3.1:8b", "licence": "llama3.1", "gb": 4.9, "vision": False, "multimodal_model": False},
    {"name": "llama3.2-vision:11b", "licence": "llama3.2", "gb": 7.8, "vision": True, "multimodal_model": True},
    {"name": "muse-glimmer:30b", "licence": "apache-2.0", "gb": 18.0, "vision": True, "multimodal_model": True},
    {"name": "llama3.3:70b", "licence": "llama3.3", "gb": 43.0, "vision": False, "multimodal_model": False},
    {"name": "llama4:scout", "licence": "llama4", "gb": 67.0, "vision": True, "multimodal_model": True},
]

def eu_rights(model, entity):
    if not model["licence"].startswith("llama"):
        return "granted"
    restricted = model["multimodal_model"] and model["licence"] in {"llama3.2", "llama4"}
    if restricted and entity.get("eu") and not entity.get("end_user_only"):
        return "not granted"
    return "granted"

BY_NAME = {m["name"]: m for m in CATALOGUE}
print([eu_rights(m, {"eu": True}) for m in CATALOGUE])
print(eu_rights(BY_NAME["llama4:scout"], {"eu": True, "end_user_only": True}))
```

- **For an EU company, two models are not granted: Llama 3.2 Vision and Llama 4 Scout.**
- **Muse Glimmer is multimodal but Apache 2.0,** so the Llama restriction does not apply.
- **End users of a product are exempt.** The restriction is about who builds with the model, not who uses the finished product.

Then, the obligations per licence.

```python
OBLIGATIONS = {
    "apache-2.0": ["include the licence", "keep notices", "mark changes"],
    "llama": ["include the licence", 'display "Built with Llama"', 'name derived models "Llama-"',
              "follow the acceptable use policy", "700M MAU clause"],
}

def obligations(model):
    return OBLIGATIONS["llama" if model["licence"].startswith("llama") else model["licence"]]

print(obligations(BY_NAME["muse-glimmer:30b"]))
print(obligations(BY_NAME["llama3.3:70b"]))
```

- **Three obligations for Apache 2.0, five for Llama.** The list is a checklist for release reviews.
- **All Llama 3.x and 4 versions share the structure,** though each has its own agreement text and policy to check.

Finally, choosing a model under memory and licence constraints.

```python
def choose(catalogue, gpu_gb, need_vision, entity, headroom=1.2):
    best = None
    for m in catalogue:
        if need_vision and not m["vision"]:
            continue
        if eu_rights(m, entity) != "granted":
            continue
        if m["gb"] * headroom > gpu_gb:
            continue
        if best is None or m["gb"] > best["gb"]:
            best = m
    return best["name"] if best else None

UK, EU = {"eu": False}, {"eu": True}
print(choose(CATALOGUE, 16, True, UK), choose(CATALOGUE, 16, True, EU), choose(CATALOGUE, 24, True, EU))
print(choose(CATALOGUE, 64, False, UK), choose(CATALOGUE, 96, True, EU))
```

- **A UK company with 16 GB and a vision need gets Llama 3.2 Vision 11B; an EU company gets nothing.**
- **With 24 GB, the EU company can use Muse Glimmer,** which needs 21.6 GB with headroom and carries no EU restriction.
- **With 64 GB and no vision need, Llama 3.3 70B fits.** With 96 GB and vision, an EU company still cannot use Llama 4 Scout and gets Muse Glimmer.
- **"Largest that fits" is a crude quality proxy.** Test the candidates, Module 10.15.

**Complexity.** Constant per check; linear in the catalogue for the choice.

## 4. Practitioner Wisdom & Edge Cases (Reddit / HN / Stack Overflow Synthesis)

**Calling Llama "open source".** It is open weights under a restrictive licence. Say what it is.

**Forgetting the naming rule for distillations.** Training on Llama outputs counts. Name the model "Llama-" first.

**Skipping "Built with Llama".** A product page or documentation mention is enough. Add it.

**EU teams building with vision Llama models.** Not granted for multimodal 3.2 and 4. Choose another family.

**Assuming Llama 4 runs on a gaming PC.** Scout is 67 GB at 4 bits. Use 3.x or another family on small machines.

**Trusting 10M-token context.** Advertised and useful context differ. Test long-context tasks yourself.

**Benchmark controversies.** Leaderboard versions may differ from released weights. Test the weights you will run.

**Assuming Meta's newest open model is Llama-licensed.** Muse Glimmer is Apache 2.0. Check each licence.

**Fine-tunes inherit the licence.** A Llama-based model on Hugging Face carries Llama's conditions. Check the base model.

**Image tasks in other languages.** Llama 3.2 Vision is English-only for image plus text. Test multilingual image needs.

**Internal use still has rules.** The use policy applies even without distribution.

**Licence texts differ by version.** Read the agreement for the exact version you use.

    """Redistribution duties: licence copy, "Built with Llama", and derived-model naming."""
