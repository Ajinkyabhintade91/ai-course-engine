# YouTube Videos

Long-form video is the most demanding format in this unit and the one that rewards the work most. A ten-minute video can teach a skill properly, show a whole project, or make an argument that a Reel never could. It is also where an audience comes to know someone, because they spend real time with them.

Everything earlier in the unit feeds into it. The pipeline of Module 6.1, the script of Module 6.6, the voice of Module 6.4, the images and footage of Modules 6.2 and 6.3, and the thumbnail of Module 6.7 all meet in one upload. This chapter covers the workflow that ties them together, the packaging around the video, title, description, and chapters, that helps it be found, and the analytics that say what to do differently next time. AI helps at nearly every stage, and the chapter is careful about where it should not.

## 1. First-Principles Mental Model & Intuition

### A promise, kept for ten minutes

A long video makes a promise in its title and thumbnail and keeps it for as long as it runs. The platform watches two things closely: whether people choose to start it, and whether they are glad they did. Its own explanation of how recommendations work names satisfaction as part of what it measures, alongside what people click and how long they watch.

That gives the whole job a simple shape.

```text
   PACKAGING                  THE VIDEO                      THE DATA
   title + thumbnail   ->     delivers on the promise   ->   who started, who stayed,
   "is this for me?"          minute by minute                where they left
         ^                                                          |
         +------------------ what you learn changes ---------------+
                               the next video's packaging and script
```

### The funnel

Every view comes through a funnel. The platform shows the thumbnail to some number of people, the **impressions**. A share of them click, the **click-through rate**. Those who click watch for some time, the **average view duration**. Multiplied together, these give the watch time the video earned:

```text
   impressions  x  click-through rate  =  views
        views   x  average view duration  =  watch time
```

The two halves are the two halves of the job. Packaging moves the click-through rate. The video itself moves the view duration. A video can fail at either, and the fix is different in each case.

### Where AI fits

| Stage | AI is good at | Keep for yourself |
|---|---|---|
| Research | Summarising sources, finding what people ask | Choosing what you have to add |
| Packaging | Ten title options, thumbnail concepts | Picking the true and compelling one |
| Script | Outlines, drafts, tightening | The argument, the stories, the facts |
| Recording | Teleprompter scripts, AI voice where suitable | Being on camera, if that is the format |
| Editing | Transcribing, removing pauses and filler, rough cuts | Pace, what to leave out, the final cut |
| Publishing | Descriptions, chapters, translations, captions | Checking every line before it goes out |
| Analysis | Summarising the numbers and comments | Deciding what to change |

## 2. Core Theory, Mathematical Foundations & Architecture Internals

### The workflow

| Stage | Output | Gate before the next stage |
|---|---|---|
| **Research** | Notes, sources, the question the video answers | Is there something here only you can say? |
| **Package first** | A working title and a thumbnail concept | Would you click it? Can the video deliver it? |
| **Script** | A script in the structure of Module 6.6 | Facts checked, read aloud |
| **Record or generate** | Footage and narration | Sound clean, every point covered |
| **Edit** | A cut with captions and graphics | Watched through once at normal speed |
| **Package for real** | Final title, thumbnail, description, chapters | All checks passed |
| **Publish and analyse** | The video live, then its numbers | One lesson recorded for the next video |

Two features of this order matter.

**Package first.** Deciding the title and thumbnail before the script forces the question every viewer will ask: why watch this? If no title is both honest and compelling, the video does not yet have a point. Many experienced creators work this way, and it has a practical benefit: the script can be written to deliver exactly what the packaging promises, so that the opening matches what was clicked, as Module 6.6 required.

**Edit with a transcript.** Editing tools that work from a transcript let you cut video by deleting words on a page. Removing a stumble, a repeated take, or a tangent becomes a text edit. Many such tools also remove long silences and filler words automatically. It is the largest time saving AI has brought to long-form editing.

### The first thirty seconds

YouTube's analytics report what share of viewers are still watching at thirty seconds, which it calls the intro, and its help pages advise making the opening match what the title and thumbnail promised. Module 6.6 covered how to write that opening. For long videos two habits help in production. Cut the first take of the opening and record it again at the end of the session, when you are warmed up. And remove everything before the first sentence that matters: logos, greetings, and "before we start" go, or move to later.

### Pauses and pace

Recorded speech is full of gaps: breaths, pauses to think, the silence while a hand reaches for a tool. A few are natural. Many make a video drag. A common editing rule is to shorten every gap longer than some limit down to that limit. If a recording has gaps $g_{1}, g_{2}, \dots$ between spoken segments, and the limit is $m$, the time saved is

$$
\sum_{i} \max(0,\; g_{i} - m)
$$

A ten-minute recording often loses a minute or more this way, with nothing said removed. The lab builds the operation. The judgement it cannot make is which pauses were deliberate, a beat before a reveal, and those should be restored by hand.

### Titles, descriptions, and chapters

| Element | Limits and rules | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Title** | Up to 100 characters. Long titles are cut off in many places, so put the important words early | Tells people and the search system what the video is |
| **Description** | Up to 5,000 characters. Only the first lines show without expanding | Context, links, chapters, and the words people search for |
| **Chapters** | Timestamps in the description, as below | Lets viewers jump to a part, and shows them the structure |
| **Tags** | Allowed, with a minor role | Mainly help with common misspellings of the topic |

YouTube's help pages give the rules for chapters added by hand. The timestamps go in the description, each followed by a title. The first must be at 0:00. There must be at least three, in ascending order, and each chapter must be at least 10 seconds long. When these conditions are met the progress bar is divided into labelled sections, and the platform can also generate chapters automatically when none are given.

Chapters help viewers in two ways. Someone looking for one step can jump to it instead of leaving. And the list of chapters, read before the video starts, is itself a summary that tells a viewer the video is worth their time.

### How the video is found

YouTube's own explanation says that search results weigh relevance, engagement, and quality, and that recommendations draw on what people watch, how long, what they like and dislike, and responses to surveys about satisfaction. In practice:

- **Relevance** comes from the title, description, and the words spoken, which the platform can transcribe. Use the words people would search for, naturally.
- **Engagement** comes from the funnel: whether people click, and how long they watch.
- **Quality and satisfaction** come from whether viewers are glad they watched, which no keyword can fake.

Optimising the packaging can get a video seen. Only the video can make it recommended again.

### The funnel, in numbers

If a video gets $I$ impressions with click-through rate $c$ and average view duration $d$ minutes:

$$
\text{views} = I \times c \qquad \text{watch hours} = \frac{I \times c \times d}{60}
$$

The trade-off of Module 6.7 appears again. A video with an 8 percent click rate watched for 2 minutes earns less than one with a 5 percent rate watched for 4. For 50,000 impressions, the first earns 133 watch hours and the second 167. A third, with a 4 percent rate and a 6.5-minute average, earns 217. The video people watch longest wins, even with the fewest clicks.

### Reading the analytics

| Metric | What it says | If it is low |
|---|---|---|
| **Impressions** | How often the platform showed the thumbnail | The topic or early performance did not earn distribution |
| **Impressions click-through rate** | How compelling the packaging was | Rework title and thumbnail |
| **Average view duration** | How long a typical viewer stayed | Opening, pace, or broken promise |
| **Average percentage viewed** | Duration as a share of the length | Compare with your other videos of similar length |
| **Audience retention graph** | Where viewers left, second by second | Read the script at the steepest drop |
| **Returning versus new viewers** | Whether people come back | Consistency, and whether you give a reason to return |

Module 6.6 showed how to read the retention graph. Compare each video with your own earlier ones of similar length and topic, since every channel's numbers are different.

### AI features and rules on the platform

YouTube has added AI tools for creators inside its studio: an assistant that answers questions about a channel's performance, automatic dubbing into other languages, and likeness detection that helps creators find videos using their face without permission. Features of this kind arrive and change frequently. Check what is available in your studio, and treat any AI-written title or description as a draft.

Two policies bear directly on AI-assisted long-form video.

**Inauthentic content.** In July 2025 YouTube clarified its monetisation policy: mass-produced or repetitive content that looks template-made is not eligible to earn money, and that includes AI content built on generic templates without original insight. A shared intro and outro are fine if the bulk of each video differs.

**Disclosure.** Realistic altered or synthetic content must be declared when uploading. Using AI for a script, ideas, a title, or a thumbnail does not require it, and the platform's own examples say cloning your own voice for dubbing does not either. Repeatedly failing to disclose can lead to labels, penalties, and removal from the partner programme.

### Complexity

Every operation in the lab is linear in the number of chapters, segments, or characters. The real cost of a long video is hours of recording and editing, and the operations that save the most, transcript editing and pause removal, are linear passes that a machine does in seconds.

## 3. Production-Grade Implementation & Annotated Walkthrough

First, chapters: writing them, and checking them.

```python
import re

def format_timestamp(seconds):
    seconds = int(seconds)
    hours, remainder = divmod(seconds, 3600)
    minutes, secs = divmod(remainder, 60)
    return f"{hours}:{minutes:02d}:{secs:02d}" if hours else f"{minutes}:{secs:02d}"

def build_chapters(chapters):
    return "\n".join(f"{format_timestamp(start)} {title}" for start, title in chapters)

STAMP = re.compile(r"^\s*(?:(\d+):)?(\d{1,2}):(\d{2})\s+(\S.*)$")

def parse_chapters(description):
    found = []
    for line in description.splitlines():
        match = STAMP.match(line)
        if match:
            hours, minutes, seconds, title = match.groups()
            found.append((int(hours or 0) * 3600 + int(minutes) * 60 + int(seconds), title.strip()))
    return found

def chapter_problems(description, video_seconds):
    chapters, problems = parse_chapters(description), []
    if len(chapters) < 3:
        problems.append("fewer-than-3")
    if chapters and chapters[0][0] != 0:
        problems.append("first-not-zero")
    starts = [start for start, _ in chapters]
    if starts != sorted(starts) or len(set(starts)) != len(starts):
        problems.append("not-ascending")
    ends = starts[1:] + [video_seconds]
    if any(end - start < 10 for start, end in zip(starts, ends)):
        problems.append("chapter-under-10s")
    return problems

CHAPTERS = [(0, "Why the chisel would not cut"), (42, "Flatten the back"), (155, "Set the bevel"),
            (318, "The mistake that ruins edges"), (402, "Strop and test")]
DESCRIPTION = "How to sharpen a chisel bought for four pounds.\n\n" + build_chapters(CHAPTERS) + "\n\nTools: two stones and an old belt."
print(build_chapters(CHAPTERS))
print(chapter_problems(DESCRIPTION, 480))
print(chapter_problems("0:05 Intro\n1:00 Back\n0:50 Bevel\n7:55 End", 480))
print([format_timestamp(value) for value in (9, 61, 3599, 3725)])
```

- **`format_timestamp` writes seconds as the platform expects.** Minutes and two-digit seconds, with hours added in front only when needed: 61 seconds is "1:01", and 3,725 is "1:02:05".
- **`build_chapters` writes one line per chapter.** These lines go in the description, and the platform turns them into sections of the progress bar.
- **`parse_chapters` reads them back.** It finds lines that start with a timestamp and a title anywhere in the description, ignoring the rest.
- **`chapter_problems` applies the four rules from Section 2.** At least three chapters, the first at zero, ascending order, and every chapter at least ten seconds long. The last chapter runs to the end of the video, which is why its length is given.
- **The chisel video's chapters pass.** Five chapters, starting at zero, each far longer than ten seconds.
- **The broken example fails three rules.** It starts at five seconds, it goes backwards from 1:00 to 0:50, and its last chapter is five seconds long. On the platform none of these chapters would appear.

Next, shortening pauses.

```python
SEGMENTS = [(0.0, 3.2, "This chisel cost four pounds."), (3.9, 6.8, "It could not cut butter."),
            (9.5, 13.0, "Let me show you why."), (13.4, 20.1, "First, the back."),
            (24.6, 30.0, "Twenty strokes on the coarse stone.")]

def tighten(segments, max_gap=0.5):
    tightened, clock, previous_end = [], 0.0, None
    for start, end, text in segments:
        if previous_end is not None:
            clock += min(start - previous_end, max_gap)
        length = end - start
        tightened.append((round(clock, 2), round(clock + length, 2), text))
        clock += length
        previous_end = end
    if not segments:
        return {"segments": [], "saved": 0.0}
    original = segments[-1][1] - segments[0][0]
    return {"segments": tightened, "saved": round(original - (tightened[-1][1] - tightened[0][0]), 2)}

result = tighten(SEGMENTS)
for segment in result["segments"]:
    print(segment)
print(result["saved"])
```

- **The recording is a list of spoken segments.** Each has a start, an end, and its words, as a transcription tool produces.
- **Every gap is capped at half a second.** The gap of 0.7 seconds after the first line becomes 0.5. The gap of 2.7 seconds while reaching for a stone becomes 0.5. The gap of 4.5 seconds before the strokes becomes 0.5.
- **The spoken segments keep their length.** Only silence is removed, and each segment is shifted earlier by the silence removed before it.
- **Thirty seconds become 23.6.** The function saved 6.4 seconds, more than a fifth, without cutting a word.
- **Not every pause is a mistake.** A pause before "Let me show you why" may be the beat that makes it land. The tool does the bulk of the work and an editor puts back the pauses that were meant.

Then, the funnel.

```python
def funnel(impressions, ctr, avg_view_minutes):
    views = impressions * ctr
    return {"views": round(views), "watch_hours": round(views * avg_view_minutes / 60, 1)}

for label, ctr, minutes in (("clickbait", 0.08, 2.0), ("solid", 0.05, 4.0), ("deep", 0.04, 6.5)):
    print(label, funnel(50000, ctr, minutes))
```

- **The function multiplies down the funnel.** Impressions times the click-through rate is views. Views times the average view duration, divided by sixty, is watch hours.
- **The "clickbait" version gets the most views, 4,000, and the fewest watch hours, 133.** People clicked and left.
- **The "solid" version gets 2,500 views and 167 watch hours.**
- **The "deep" version gets the fewest views, 2,000, and the most watch hours, 217.** Fewer people, each staying for six and a half minutes.
- **The platform's interest is in the last column.** It is also the honest measure of whether a video helped anyone.

Finally, checking the title and description before upload.

```python
def metadata_problems(title, description, keyword):
    problems, key = [], keyword.lower()
    if not title.strip():
        problems.append("no-title")
    elif len(title) > 100:
        problems.append("title-too-long")
    if key and key not in title.lower()[:60]:
        problems.append("keyword-late-in-title")
    if len(description) > 5000:
        problems.append("description-too-long")
    if key and key not in description.lower()[:150]:
        problems.append("keyword-not-early-in-description")
    if len(parse_chapters(description)) < 3:
        problems.append("no-chapters")
    return problems

GOOD_TITLE = "How to Sharpen a Chisel: From Four-Pound Junk to Razor Sharp"
VLOG_TITLE = ("My Workshop Vlog Episode 47 - lots of things happened this week including "
              "finally learning how to sharpen a chisel properly")
print(len(GOOD_TITLE), metadata_problems(GOOD_TITLE, DESCRIPTION, "sharpen a chisel"))
print(metadata_problems(VLOG_TITLE, "x" * 5001, "sharpen a chisel"))
```

- **Six checks, from the limits in Section 2 and two working values.** A title exists and is within 100 characters. The search phrase appears in the first 60 characters of the title, where it will not be cut off. The description is within 5,000 characters, has the phrase in its first 150, which show before the viewer expands it, and contains chapters.
- **The tutorial's title is 60 characters with the phrase near the start.** All six checks pass.
- **The vlog title fails five.** It is too long, the phrase comes at the end where it would be cut off, the description is over the limit and does not mention the topic early, and there are no chapters.
- **60 and 150 are working values.** How much of a title shows depends on the device and the page. The principle, that the important words come first, does not.
- **Passing the checks does not make a video findable.** It removes reasons for it not to be. Whether people click and stay decides the rest.

**Complexity.** Each function is linear in the number of chapters, segments, or characters.

### An upload checklist

```text
TITLE        [under 100 characters, the topic in the first 60, true to the video]
THUMBNAIL    [passes the checks of Module 6.7]
DESCRIPTION  First two lines: what the viewer gets, with the words they would search for
             Chapters: 0:00 first, at least 3, ascending, each at least 10 s
             Links, credits, and any sponsor disclosure
CAPTIONS     Generated, then corrected: names, numbers, terms
DISCLOSURE   Realistic synthetic content? [yes / no, and why]
END SCREEN   One next video, chosen for this viewer
AFTER 7 DAYS Intro retention, steepest drop, click-through rate vs your median, one lesson
```

### Diagnosing a video

| Click rate | View duration | Diagnosis | Next time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | High | Good video, weak packaging | Rework title and thumbnail. Test with the platform's feature |
| High | Low | Packaging over-promised, or a slow opening | Match the opening to the promise. Cut the first 30 seconds harder |
| Low | Low | The topic or the execution missed | A different topic, or a different angle on it |
| High | High | Working | Make the next one on the same pattern |

## 4. Practitioner Wisdom & Edge Cases (Reddit / HN / Stack Overflow Synthesis)

**Packaging last.** A video made first and titled afterwards often turns out to have no clear promise. Decide what the viewer gets, title it, and make the video that delivers it.

**A slow opening.** A minute of introduction, sponsor message, and "make sure you subscribe" before the content starts is where most long videos lose most viewers. Begin with the reason they clicked.

**Uncut pauses and repeated takes.** Footage edited in the order it was recorded drags. Transcript-based editing and pause removal take out the dead air in minutes. Then watch it through at normal speed to restore the pauses that were meant.

**Trusting generated captions.** Automatic captions mishear names, numbers, and trade terms. They are read by people with the sound off and by search systems. Correct them.

**Chapters that break the rules.** A first timestamp at 0:05, or two chapters a few seconds apart, and the platform shows none of them. Start at 0:00, keep each at least ten seconds, keep them in order.

**Keyword stuffing.** Repeating a search phrase in the title, description, and tags reads as spam to viewers and does nothing the platform cannot see through. Use the words people search for, once, naturally.

**AI titles taken as given.** Generated titles drift towards the generic and the exaggerated. Use them as a list to choose from and rewrite, and check the result against what the video actually shows.

**Judging by views.** A video with many views and a short average duration did worse than it looks. Read watch time and retention, and compare with your own past videos.

**Changing everything at once.** A new title, a new thumbnail, and a new description on the same day leave no way to tell which helped. Change one, or use the platform's test feature for title and thumbnail.

**Template channels.** Channels that publish AI-narrated slideshows on a formula fall under the inauthentic content policy and are not eligible for monetisation. AI that helps you make your own videos is a different thing from AI that makes interchangeable ones.

**Forgetting disclosure.** A realistic synthetic person or scene in a video must be declared at upload. Your own voice cloned for a dubbed version need not be, and a generated thumbnail need not be. Read the current rules.

**No lesson recorded.** Each video's analytics hold one useful lesson, usually about the opening or the packaging. Write it down before starting the next, or the next video repeats the mistake.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 1
def build_chapters(chapters):
    """Chapters let a viewer jump to the part they want instead of leaving, and the list
    itself tells them before they start that the video is worth their time."""
    return "\n".join(f"{format_timestamp(start)} {title}" for start, title in chapters)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 2
def chapter_problems(description, video_seconds):
    """Any one broken rule and the platform shows no chapters at all."""
    chapters, problems = parse_chapters(description), []
    if len(chapters) < 3:
        problems.append("fewer-than-3")
    if chapters and chapters[0][0] != 0:
        problems.append("first-not-zero")
    starts = [start for start, _ in chapters]
    if starts != sorted(starts) or len(set(starts)) != len(starts):
        problems.append("not-ascending")
    ends = starts[1:] + [video_seconds]
    if any(end - start < 10 for start, end in zip(starts, ends)):
        problems.append("chapter-under-10s")
    return problems


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 3
def tighten(segments, max_gap=0.5):
    """Silence is removed and every word is kept. An editor then restores the pauses that
    were meant, such as the beat before a reveal."""
    tightened, clock, previous_end = [], 0.0, None
    for start, end, text in segments:
        if previous_end is not None:
            clock += min(start - previous_end, max_gap)
        length = end - start
        tightened.append((round(clock, 2), round(clock + length, 2), text))
        clock += length
        previous_end = end
    if not segments:
        return {"segments": [], "saved": 0.0}
    original = segments[-1][1] - segments[0][0]
    return {"segments": tightened, "saved": round(original - (tightened[-1][1] - tightened[0][0]), 2)}


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- Step 4
def funnel(impressions, ctr, avg_view_minutes):
    """Packaging moves the click rate and the video moves the view duration. Watch time is
    their product, and the video people watch longest wins."""
    views = impressions * ctr
    return {"views": round(views), "watch_hours": round(views * avg_view_minutes / 60, 1)}

    the rest."""
    problems, key = [], keyword.lower()
